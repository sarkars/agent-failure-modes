# Why People Leave Chatbots: An Evidence Review

> **Status:** draft v0.1, written 2026-10-19. Every study below was opened for this review or for the linked pattern page, and each row says what was and was not read. Where a pattern page records a source as read only through a summary, it is listed as unverified here and carries no weight. Nothing here is our own data: no study from our [analytics plan](../chat-analytics-plan.md) has been run.

## The question
When people abandon a chatbot conversation, what do opened studies and regulator reports say causes it? And what do they say about whether persuasion and conversion design can change the outcome? This review is a map of the evidence we hold, with its limits. It is chat-first; voice studies are marked.

## What is not in the evidence
- **No measured drop-off rate across chatbots.** We found no open dataset of how many conversations end in abandonment, or why.
- **No A/B test of the fixes in our [patterns](../../README.md).** The fixes are reasoned from the failures and the research below; the tests in `tests/` are all "Not yet run".
- **Few studies of text chat in the wild.** Most evidence is one bank, one platform, a survey, or a regulator's reading of complaints.

## Part 1: Causes of abandonment, by what the evidence supports
| Cause | What the opened sources say | Source (status) | Limits | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **No route to a person** | The CFPB described customers stuck in "continuous loops of repetitive, unhelpful jargon or legalese without an offramp to a human customer service representative" and called them "doom loops". | [CFPB, *Chatbots in consumer finance*, June 2023](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_chatbot-issue-spotlight_2023-06.pdf) (opened and read) | US consumer finance; quotes complaints, gives no count | [Talk to a human ignored or looped](../../goals/human-handoff/failures/talk-to-a-human-ignored-or-looped.md) |
| **The bot does not make progress** | In a three-month log of 1,685 users of a banking chatbot, researchers found 12 types of non-progress and signs that users were about to abandon: three consecutive non-progress turns, consecutive reformulation or subject switching, and reformulating as the final strategy. | [Li et al., CHI 2020](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~armuro/pubs/li-et-al-2020-chi.pdf) (opened; abstract and introduction read) | One bank, one chatbot; observational | [Dead-end "I don't understand" replies](../../goals/conversation-leadership/failures/dead-end-i-don-t-understand-replies.md) |
| **Abandonment can be predicted from those signals** | A model on an annotated log of 1,837 users predicted abandonment (AUROC 90% for a BERT model, 95.7% for a feature-based model). | [Yang et al., CUI 2021](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~armuro/pubs/yang-et-al-2021-cui-ea.pdf) (abstract opened) | Prediction on one bank's log; does not show any fix works | Same |
| **Repair offered badly** | In a scenario study with 203 workers comparing eight repair strategies, offering options and explanations was generally favored. | [Ashktorab et al., CHI 2019](https://research.ibm.com/publications/resilient-chatbots-repair-strategy-preferences-for-conversational-breakdowns) (abstract opened) | Hypothetical scenarios, not live use | Same |
| **Silence, stalls, and failures with no message** | Response-time guidance says feedback is needed past about 10 seconds. Vendor guidance says streamed answers can be cut off by timeouts or network failures and recommends detecting a missing finish signal. | [Nielsen, NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/); [Atlassian Forge docs](https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/runtime-reference/forge-llms-api-errors/) (both opened; Atlassian read 2026-10-19) | General interface guidance and one vendor; no measured effect on abandonment | [Silent long-running work](../../goals/response-time/failures/silent-long-running-work.md), [Silent failure and timeout](../../goals/response-time/failures/silent-failure-and-timeout.md) |
| **Being asked for what the bot should already know** | A Salesforce survey (Australia and New Zealand, 535 customers) found 66% often repeat information to different representatives. | [B&T article on the report](https://www.bandt.com.au/customers-expect-connected-experience-still-dealing-silos/) (article opened; the report itself was not) | Vendor survey; about people, not bots | [Re-asks for known information](../../goals/embedded-copilots/failures/re-asks-for-known-information.md) |
| **Stated attitudes toward AI in service** | A Gartner survey reportedly found 64% prefer companies not use AI in customer service. | Gartner press release, July 2024 (**read through a search summary only; unverified, not used**) | Survey of attitudes, not behavior | n/a |

**What the table says:** the strongest evidence is for the dead end and the missing route to a person. For the rest, the evidence is guidance or small studies, so the patterns present them as likely causes to test, not proven drivers of drop-off.

## Part 2: Does disclosing a bot, or making it persuasive, change outcomes?
Three studies bear on the "boring bot" and conversion question. All three were opened for this review.

| Study | What it reports | Source (status) | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luo, Tong, Fang and Qu (2019)** | A field experiment on more than 6,200 customers randomized to outbound sales calls from chatbots or human workers. Undisclosed chatbots were as effective as proficient workers and four times as effective as inexperienced workers; disclosing the chatbot's identity before the conversation reduced purchase rates by more than 79.7%. Customers who knew it was a machine were curt and perceived it as less knowledgeable and less empathetic. The authors report that a late disclosure timing and customers' prior AI experience reduced the negative effect. | *Marketing Science* 38(6), 937-947, [abstract on IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v38y2019i6p937-947.html) (opened; abstract read) | Highly structured outbound sales calls, not open chat. Disclosure rules and customer attitudes may have changed since 2019. We cannot say "do not disclose": disclosure may be required by law in some places, and that is a question for counsel. |
| **Xu, Dai and Yan (Management Science, 2025 early access)** | In a field experiment with a large truck-sharing platform in Asia, 11,000 truck drivers were randomly assigned to outbound calls from a voice chatbot dispatcher. Disclosing the chatbot's identity at the start led to about an 11% reduction in response probability. Adding interjections and filler words raised response probability, conversation length, and order acceptance intention by over 5.6%, 24.9%, and 10.1%. With disclosure and these features together, outcomes still improved. | [Seminar abstract (SUFE/GLOBE listing)](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/386643) and [UNC Kenan-Flagler article](https://www.kenan-flagler.unc.edu/news/making-voice-chatbots-more-effective-by-design/) (both opened; I did not open the journal page, which refused access) | **Voice**, not text chat. One platform and one population. Figures come from the abstract; I did not read the paper. A separate listing gives a different upper range for one effect, so quote the abstract's figures only. |
| **Salvi, Horta Ribeiro, Gallotti and West (Nature Human Behaviour, 2025)** | A preregistered study (N = 900) of short multiround debates, randomized across human or GPT-4 opponent, with or without access to the participant's sociodemographic data, and three topic opinion strengths. In debate pairs where AI and humans were not equally persuasive, GPT-4 with personalization was more persuasive 64.4% of the time (81.2% relative increase in odds of higher post-debate agreement; 95% CI +26.0% to +160.7%). | [Nature Human Behaviour article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02194-6) (opened; abstract read). An earlier [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14380) reports 81.7% higher odds with N = 820; use the journal figures. | Debate on opinion topics in a controlled setting, not selling or support. The authors frame it as a governance and misuse concern. It shows personalization can raise persuasion in debate, not that it raises conversion in a business chat. |

**What this means for design, with limits.**
- The two field experiments both show that telling people they are talking to a machine lowers response or purchase in those settings. Neither tells you to hide it. Honest disclosure plus better conversation design is the position the Xu result supports, and it is voice evidence.
- Style choices (filler words in voice, empathy, timing of disclosure) moved outcomes in those studies. That supports testing wording and flow as variables, which is the job of the [analytics plan's studies](../chat-analytics-plan.md). It does not give us a recipe for text chat.
- Persuasion research is an argument for guardrails as much as for conversion. Our persuasion layer, if built, is gated by disclosure and consent rules ([distribution plan](../distribution-plan.md)).

## Part 3: What to publish and what to test next
1. Publish this page with its limits, as the first public statement of what is and is not known.
2. Use Part 1 to choose which failures the [studies S0-S6](../chat-analytics-plan.md) test first: dead ends and the missing route to a person have the strongest prior evidence, so a simulated study can ask whether our proposed detection signals find them.
3. Do not claim any conversion result. We have none.
