# Roadmap: Emerging Surfaces (What's Growing in the Market)

> **Family goal:** New ways of using agents (buying, browsing, tool ecosystems, meetings, companions, hiring, outcome-priced support) are checked against the same human-interface standards as chat and voice: honest, authorised, safe, and fair.

**How I chose these.** I searched for what's growing now (October 2026) and kept only areas with at least one source I could cite, flagging weak ones. The market figures below are **directional**: most come from vendor, trade, and analyst-blog sources, and definitions differ.

**Market signals (verified through search, mostly secondary):**
- **Enterprise copilots and agents:** Gartner expects 40% of enterprise applications to include task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from under 5% a year earlier. A March 2026 report found fewer than 10% of enterprise functions have scaled agents.
- **Agentic commerce:** Juniper Research estimates $8 billion of transactions in 2026, rising to $3.5 trillion by 2031. Protocols from OpenAI and Stripe, and from Google, define how agents carry out purchases, and card networks have announced agent-payment programmes. Dates and details vary by source.
- **Voice agents:** one source calls voice the fastest-growing channel, though chat still leads by a wide margin.
- **AI browsers:** prompt injection is the central unsolved problem for browser agents such as ChatGPT Atlas and Perplexity Comet.
- **Tool ecosystems:** reports describe dozens of vulnerabilities in tool-protocol servers and clients in early 2026, with counts differing by source.
- **Pricing:** several customer-support vendors bill per resolution (Intercom's Fin at about $0.99 per outcome is the best-supported figure), so the definition of "resolved" now drives revenue.
- **Ads in assistants:** see [embedded copilots](embedded-copilots.md) (CP-05).

Flags: **V** verified source · **R** needs research · **L** legal review · **E** expert review. **In the library today:** **Covered** = a pattern already exists, so no new one is planned · **Extends** = an existing pattern covers the technical failure and a new one would add the experience or design angle and a fix · **New** = no matching pattern found by file-name and keyword search (re-check before writing). See the [roadmap README](README.md). **Root cause:** Model = a stronger model plausibly reduces it · Reliability = state, verification, enforcement, evals, latency · Business context = your data, entitlements, rules, workflows, UX · Governance = incentives, law, and decisions. **Better model alone?** Yes / Partly / No. These two columns are my judgments (one label per row), not measurements.
