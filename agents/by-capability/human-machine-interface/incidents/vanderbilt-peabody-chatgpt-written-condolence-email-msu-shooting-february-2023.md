# Vanderbilt's Peabody College Sends Students a ChatGPT-Paraphrased Message After the Michigan State Shooting, February 2023

**This page is a dated teardown of one public case, a university office that sent students a message of support after a campus shooting with text paraphrased from ChatGPT, for teams whose staff or products send AI-drafted messages to people in grief or shock.** The sender was the Peabody College Office of Equity, Diversity and Inclusion at Vanderbilt University in Nashville. The readers were Peabody students and staff. The moment was the days after the shooting at Michigan State University (MSU). Every fact below comes from a source we opened (listed at the end).

**Status: Needs review.** Four sources were opened on 2026-10-26 and read through a fetch tool that returns a summary of the page, not the full text: two reports by The Vanderbilt Hustler (the student paper, which broke the story), a CNN report (read as syndicated by KTVZ), and an ABC News report (read on ABC13's site). A second check on 2026-10-26 downloaded the raw pages of both Hustler reports and the CNN copy and found these in the page text: the note's wording, "poor judgment", three students killed and five injured, the February 16 email date, the February 18 step-back and the April 11 return. Other quotes are as the fetch tool returned them. The email itself, the apology email, and the dean's statements were read only as these outlets quote or describe them.

> **Read this first.** This is a summary of public reporting. It is not a finding that any person did wrong. No opened source says how much of the email a person wrote or changed. ABC News says Associate Dean Nicole Joseph did not tell it how much was human paraphrase and how much came from ChatGPT's first draft. Vanderbilt said it does not discuss employment matters, and no opened source reports what its review found. **Last updated: 2026-10-26.**

## What happened (as reported)

- **Before the email.** The Hustler says Vice Provost and Dean of Students G. L. Black emailed students about the shooting on February 14, 2023, with no ChatGPT note. ABC News says Black's message listed campus resources for students. CNN and the Hustler (April 12) say the MSU shooting killed three students and injured five more. No opened source gives the date of the shooting.
- **The email.** The Peabody EDI office emailed students about the shooting. The Hustler's text dates it February 16; the caption on its screenshot says February 15 (see "Where the sources disagree"). Per the Hustler (April 12), it was signed by Joseph, Assistant Dean Hasina Mohyuddin and a graduate assistant.
- **The note at the bottom.** CNN says a parenthetical in smaller type at the end read: "Paraphrase from OpenAI's ChatGPT AI language model, personal communication, February 15, 2023." ABC News gives the same wording, without the date.
- **What the email said.** Per the Hustler and ABC News, it stressed a safe and inclusive environment and coming together as a community. Per the Hustler and CNN, it referred to Michigan shootings in the plural, though there was one. The Hustler says it named Peabody once and used no other Vanderbilt-specific terms. ABC News says that, unlike Black's message, it gave no list of campus resources.
- **The reaction.** The Hustler quotes several students criticizing the email, including a senior from Michigan whose sister attends MSU. CNN quotes the Hustler's editor in chief saying students were outraged and confused about why administrators turned to ChatGPT.
- **The apology, February 17.** Joseph emailed the Peabody community at 6:30 p.m. CST, per the Hustler, calling the use of ChatGPT "poor judgment." She wrote that using it for communications in a time of sorrow went against the college's values (Hustler; ABC News).
- **The university's line.** A Vanderbilt spokesperson told the Hustler the university does not use ChatGPT or other AI systems to generate messages. ABC News says a spokeswoman did not answer how often staff use ChatGPT in official messages.
- **The dean's statement.** Per a statement from Dean Camilla Benbow reported by CNN, the email did not follow Peabody's normal process, which calls for several layers of review before sending, and administrators, including her, did not know about it beforehand. Per ABC News, she apologized to "all those who deserved better from us and did not receive it."
- **Step back and return.** Joseph and Mohyuddin stepped back from their EDI duties during a review (CNN; ABC News; Hustler, April 12). The Hustler says Benbow announced on April 11, 2023 that both were back in their roles, and that she was confident the issues had been addressed. It reports no findings.

## What the students saw

A message from the office that handles inclusion at their college, sent after a mass shooting at another university. It talked in general terms about community and inclusion. It got the number of shootings wrong. It named Peabody once and, per ABC News, pointed to no help. At the bottom, in small type, a line said the text came from ChatGPT. A note meant to be honest about the drafting tool became the evidence that nobody had written to them.

## Where in the flow it broke

1. **Drafting.** The office used ChatGPT as the source of the text, per the note in the email (CNN). No opened source shows the prompt or the first draft.
2. **Editing.** The sent text still had generic content and a factual slip, shootings in the plural (Hustler; CNN). No opened source says who edited it or how much.
3. **Review.** Per Benbow, the email skipped Peabody's normal layered review, and senior administrators did not see it before it went out (CNN; Hustler, April 12).
4. **Action.** It offered no resources, per ABC News, although an earlier university email had.

## Pattern match

Taxonomy: [failure taxonomy v1](../roadmap/specs/failure-taxonomy-v1.md). Both fits are partial: the patterns are written for chatbots replying in a conversation, and here staff sent a one-way email drafted with a chatbot. The failure the readers felt is the same.

- **[Hollow empathy](../goals/emotional-intelligence/failures/hollow-empathy.md)** (`hollow-empathy`). Strong partial fit, the main one. The pattern's first cause is "a template, not a reading": asked to sound caring, a model gives the safest generic text. The email's general lines on inclusion, its single mention of Peabody, and the wrong count of shootings fit that cause. The pattern's second cause is "no tie to action": the email, per ABC News, listed no resources, so its sympathy had nothing to attach to. The pattern's third cause (repeating the same line across turns) does not apply to a single email.
- **[Stakes-blind register](../goals/stakes-calibration/failures/stakes-blind-register.md)** (`stakes-blind-register`). Partial fit. The pattern says a serious topic should change how carefully a message is made and when a person is brought in. Here a message about deaths went out, per Benbow, without the review layers Peabody's normal process requires. Partial, because the pattern is about a bot's tone and fact-checking in a live chat, not a staff sign-off process.
- **Considered and rejected.** [Undisclosed bot identity](../goals/trust-and-rapport/failures/undisclosed-bot-identity.md) (`undisclosed-bot-identity`): the AI use was disclosed in the email itself, and the disclosure is how it came to light. This case shows that a disclosure does not repair empty content. [Stock phrases](../goals/persona-and-tone/failures/stock-phrases.md) (`stock-phrases`): no opened source quotes enough of the email to show repeated stock openers. [Tone changes shipped with no regression test](../goals/measurement-and-feedback/failures/tone-changes-shipped-with-no-regression-test.md) (`tone-changes-shipped-with-no-regression-test`): about prompt changes to a deployed bot, not one message.

## What the fix would have changed

These are our proposals, drawn from the hollow-empathy and stakes-blind-register fixes, not Vanderbilt's. Each is a pass/fail check on the sent message or its send log. Status: Not yet run.

1. **Get the event right.** Input: a draft message about a named tragedy. Pass: the count, place and people match an opened news source (one shooting, at MSU). Fail: any mismatch, such as shootings in the plural for one event. This is the hollow-empathy rule to reflect the specific situation.
2. **Name the readers' situation.** Pass: the message names at least one concrete link to its readers, such as students with family or friends at MSU. Fail: only general lines on community and inclusion.
3. **Attach help.** Pass: the message lists at least one support resource and how to reach it, such as campus counseling hours and a phone number. Fail: no resource. This is the hollow-empathy rule to attach an action in the same message.
4. **Grief messages get the full review.** Input: the send log for a message about death or violence. Pass: every review step the organization's normal process requires is logged as approved before sending. Fail: fewer approvals than the process requires. This applies stakes-blind-register's rule that higher stakes mean more care and a person in the loop.
5. **No drafting tool as author.** Pass: a named person can say they wrote or rewrote every sentence and signs it. Fail: the message credits a chatbot as the source of its text. Disclosing the tool is honest; the fix is to send words a person wrote for these readers.
