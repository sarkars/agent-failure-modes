**This page is a dated teardown of one reported support breakdown, for teams that put an AI chatbot at the front of support during a planned migration, such as a card, bank, or billing system switch.** The product was customer service for Bilt, a rewards company whose credit card lets members pay rent and mortgages. The users were Bilt cardholders moved to Bilt Card 2.0 after Wells Fargo ended its partnership. The moment: in and after February 2026, members whose rent or mortgage payments were missing, bounced, or late went to customer service and, per eMarketer, "many members found only AI-powered chatbots to assist, with dayslong waits to reach human representatives." The date above is the month of the transition; the Senate letter came in May 2026.

## What happened (as reported)

- **The switch.** Per HousingWire, Wells Fargo ended its Bilt partnership early "and deactivated its version of Bilt cards in February." Customers had to move to Bilt Card 2.0, "operated under new partners Cardless and Column," or to a Wells Fargo card.
- **The payment problems.** Per eMarketer, citing Fintech Business Weekly (which we did not open), "Bilt cardholders are reporting missing, bouncing, or delayed rent and mortgage payments after attempting to pay Bilt."
- **The support channel.** Per eMarketer: "When cardholders attempted to remedy the situation with Bilt's customer service, many members found only AI-powered chatbots to assist, with dayslong waits to reach human representatives."
- **Time-sensitive stakes.** Thrifty Traveler: "Bilt's shoddy customer service – powered by an AI chatbot – led some members to spend days trying to reach a real person … all while dealing with time-sensitive financial transactions like rent and mortgage payments."
- **The company's own admission, in March.** Per Thrifty Traveler, in an open letter published in March, Bilt CEO Ankur Jain said customer service "didn't reflect the standard we set for our members." We did not open that letter.
- **The Senate letter.** Per HousingWire, Sen. Warren, ranking member of the Senate banking committee, wrote to Jain. HousingWire says Warren criticized Bilt's support, "saying the company's use of an artificial intelligence chatbot made it difficult for consumers to access human representatives," and that the letter "cited customer complaints describing long wait times and ineffective responses during the transition."
- **Questions about the chatbot.** Per PYMNTS, citing The Wall Street Journal, Warren "also sought information about the use of an artificial intelligence customer service chatbot." HousingWire says she asked for written answers by June 9, including on "chatbot usage."
- **The complaint count.** HousingWire: Warren said the transition "coincided with a 1,300% spike in complaints submitted to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) that month." The opened sources do not say how many of those complaints were about the chatbot.

## Where in the flow it broke

1. **A planned event, unplanned demand.** The migration date was known in advance. Bilt itself calls the demand "unexpectedly high." The chatbot met the surge, per the reports, without a fast path to a person.
2. **The wrong job for a chatbot.** The problems were money already moved: rent withdrawn but not delivered, mortgage payments that did not arrive. eMarketer's own takeaway is that people "are still needed to handle major, emotionally charged issues." No opened source says the chatbot could look up or trace a payment.
3. **The person was days away.** The reports describe "dayslong waits" while rent and mortgage due dates kept running. A delay of days on a rent payment is the harm, not just an annoyance.
4. **No sign the chatbot treated the migration as an outage.** No source says the chatbot told members what was known, what was being fixed, or when to check back. This is a gap in the reports, not a finding.

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run. Each can be computed from text chat logs with a topic tag, a handoff flag, and the time a person first replies.

| Signal | How to compute from logs | Counter-signal |
|---|---|---|
| Time to a person on money-moved cases | For chats tagged missing or failed payment, hours from first message to first human reply | Cases the bot fully resolved, confirmed by the member |
| Repeat contacts per payment case | Count of separate chats from one member about one payment ID | Planned follow-ups the bot or staff scheduled |
| Migration-week handoff rate | Share of chats in the migration window that request or need a person, by day | Same metric for a normal week, as a baseline |
| Payment questions the bot could not act on | Share of chats tagged payment status where the bot gave no status from the payment system | Chats where no payment ID existed |
| Status-free replies | Share of migration-topic replies with no known-issue note or next-update time | Replies on topics unrelated to the migration |

## What a team can change

Our proposals, not Bilt's. Each is a pass/fail check on a text log. Status: Not yet run.

1. **Staff people for the date, not the average.** Pass: before a dated migration, the team writes a target time to a person for payment cases and checks it daily during the window. Fail: the migration window has no written target, or the target is missed with no added staff.
2. **Route money-moved cases first.** Pass: a chat that says a payment was withdrawn but not received is tagged and sent to a person queue ahead of general questions. Fail: such a chat gets the same queue and wait as a points question.
3. **Say the wait and give a bridge.** Pass: when a person is not available now, the bot states the expected wait and offers a callback or ticket number. Fail: a handoff message with no wait time and no ticket.
4. **Show what is known.** Pass: during the migration, migration-topic replies include a known-issue note and a next-update time. Fail: a migration-topic reply with neither.
5. **Do not ask the bot to fake a trace.** Pass: if the bot cannot read payment status, it says so in the first reply and routes to a person. Fail: the bot gives general steps on a payment it cannot see.
