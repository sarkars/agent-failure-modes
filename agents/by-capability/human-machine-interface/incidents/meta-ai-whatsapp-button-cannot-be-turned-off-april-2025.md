**This page is a dated teardown of public reporting on Meta AI's arrival in WhatsApp in spring 2025, for teams that add an AI assistant to a chat app or messaging surface people already use for something else.** The product was Meta AI, Meta's chatbot, placed inside WhatsApp. The users were people opening WhatsApp to message friends and family, mostly in Europe, the UK and Australia. The moment was opening the Chats screen and finding a new AI button and an AI search prompt that had no off switch. Every fact below comes from a source we opened (listed at the end). No opened source has Meta's rollout plan, usage numbers or a count of complaints. The date above is the day of the BBC report; the rollout began weeks earlier.

**Status: Needs review.** BBC News (Graham Fraser, April 23, 2025), The Guardian (Polly Hudson, opinion column, April 6, 2025), the Evening Standard (Saqib Shah, April 9, 2025), TechRadar (Mark Wilson, April 10, 2025 and April 25, 2025), heise online (Martin Holland, English edition, April 10, 2025) and Euronews (Oceane Duboust, May 11, 2025) were downloaded as raw HTML on 2026-10-28 and read as plain text. > **Read this first.** This is a summary of public reporting and company statements, not a finding of fact and not legal advice. It does not say whether Meta or WhatsApp broke any law. Voice and web signals, such as an assistant that cannot be removed from a smart speaker or a browser toolbar, are PLANNED only: never detected or measured here.

## What happened (as reported)

- **The rollout.** heise says Meta AI was integrated into the EU versions of WhatsApp, Facebook, Messenger and Instagram "at the end of March." TechRadar (April 10) says the feature "rolled out in the US and Canada some time ago" and had recently started arriving in the EU, the UK and Australia. BBC says Meta advised that it "might not be available to you yet, even if other users in your country have access."
- **Where it sits.** BBC describes "an ever-present blue circle with pink and green splashes in the bottom right of your Chats screen," plus "a search bar at the top inviting users to 'Ask Meta AI or Search'." The Evening Standard lists three ways in: a new button in chats, typing "@MetaAI" in a message, or the search bar.
- **No off switch.** TechRadar (April 10): "The biggest complaint is that there's no way to turn Meta AI off." The Guardian column: "there is no way to remove Meta AI from WhatsApp." heise: Meta "is sticking to including it by default – without it being possible to remove it." Euronews (May 11): "The feature can't be disabled."
- **The user reaction.** BBC says users on X, Bluesky and Reddit shared their frustrations. The Guardian columnist wrote, "I have not given consent," and that "the internet is full of people ranting, mostly about their inability to deactivate it." TechRadar (April 25) says there was a vocal backlash on platforms like Reddit.
- **An EU question.** The Evening Standard and Euronews report that MEP Veronika Cifrová Ostrihoňová asked the European Commission about the feature after users contacted her. TechRadar says she questioned whether the lack of an option to remove it "aligns with EU rules." No opened source reports an answer. Kleanthi Sardeli, a NOYB data protection lawyer, told Euronews: "The fact that the feature cannot be disabled is a clear violation of the obligation of Meta to implement measures that respect user privacy by design." That is her view; she names no statute in the quoted text.
- **The first-use notice.** BBC says a long message from Meta, shown before the first question, explains what Meta AI is and calls it "optional." BBC also says the chatbot states it "can only read messages people share with it."
- **What users could do instead.** TechRadar (April 10) suggests deleting a Meta AI chat by swiping left, tapping 'more' and 'delete chat'. Euronews says users can mute the AI chat by long pressing on it. Neither removes the button.
- **A brief outage.** heise (April 10) reports the button disappeared, the AI was sometimes still reachable and sometimes missing, and Meta told heise it was a technical problem.
- **Answers once opened.** BBC's reporter asked about the weather in Glasgow and one of two links pointed to Charing Cross railway station in London. TechRadar's reporter asked if it could analyze images and it replied, "I can't understand images yet." after first asking for an upload.

## What the company said

- **To the BBC.** WhatsApp said the feature is "entirely optional" and: "We think giving people these options is a good thing and we're always listening to feedback from our users." BBC says the company likens it to other permanent options in the app, such as 'channels' and 'status'.
- **To the Evening Standard.** Joshua Breckman, Communications Director, International, WhatsApp, said: "Meta AI is like any other feature," and that "some people will love it and use it, and some people won’t — and that’s absolutely fine". He said: "The biggest question we get from people is how to turn it on." Asked about a version without AI: "There’s a non-AI version of WhatsApp if they don’t click on the button."
- **On privacy.** Breckman told the Standard, "WhatsApp can’t read your personal messages." BBC quotes Meta's guidance: "Don't share information, including sensitive topics, about others or yourself that you don't want the AI to retain and use."
- **A related setting.** TechRadar (April 25) reports WhatsApp introduced "Advanced Chat Privacy," which, when turned on, blocks contacts from "exporting chats, auto-downloading media to their phone, and using messages for AI features." TechRadar notes it does not turn off Meta AI.
- **To heise.** Meta said the April outage of the button was a technical problem. heise does not quote a Meta plan to add an off switch.

## Where in the conversation flow it broke

Based on the reports above, and only as far as they go. Each step is a place a check could have caught it.

1. **Entry without asking.** The assistant appeared on the Chats screen; no opened source says users were asked first.
2. **Placement in the main path.** The button sat on the Chats screen, and the search bar itself became an AI prompt, "Ask Meta AI or Search" (BBC). Searching your own chats now passed an AI invitation.
3. **Called optional, with no off switch.** The first-use notice called it "optional" (BBC), but the only way to not use it was not to tap it. A user who did not want it had nowhere to say so.
4. **Workarounds that miss the point.** Delete or mute the AI chat (TechRadar; Euronews) and the button stays.
5. **The reply.** The company's answer to users asking how to turn it off was that it is optional and a good thing (BBC) and that a non-AI version exists if you don't click (Standard).

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run. Text-chat signals are proposed; voice and web signals are PLANNED only and are not detected or measured. Each signal can be computed from in-app chat logs and settings events of an assistant embedded in a messaging app.

- Share of assistant conversations whose first user message asks how to turn off, remove, hide or disable the assistant.
- Share of those turn-off questions whose next assistant reply does not name a working setting or say plainly that none exists.
- Share of users who open the assistant once, send no message or only a turn-off question, and do not open it again.
- Share of searches from the chat-list search bar that the user meant for their own chats but that were routed to the assistant (the user then retypes the query or backs out).
- Share of users who see the first-use notice and dismiss it, compared with the share who later find a setting to hide the entry point, if one exists.
- Planned for voice and web only, not measured: whether an assistant added to a speaker or a browser can be turned off by the user.

## What a team can change

Our proposals, not Meta's. Each is a pass/fail check on a transcript, a settings audit or a test run. Status: Not yet run. Windows and thresholds are for the team to set.

1. **Ship the off switch with the feature.** Pass: a settings audit before launch finds a user-level control that hides the assistant's entry points in the chat list and the search bar, and a test account confirms both are gone after it is used.
2. **Make "optional" true in the notice.** Pass: the first-use notice says how to turn the assistant off, or it does not use the word "optional."
3. **Answer the turn-off question.** Pass: in a test where a user asks the assistant how to turn it off, the assistant's next reply names the exact setting path, or says plainly there is none, and does not change the subject.
4. **Keep search for chats.** Pass: in a test, a search typed in the chat-list bar returns the user's own chats first and sends nothing to the assistant unless the user picks the AI option.
5. **Remember the "no."** Pass: after a user hides the assistant, no update, banner or prompt re-adds it without asking again (see [Dismissed suggestions come back](../goals/proactive-and-ambient/failures/dismissed-suggestions-come-back.md)).
6. **Don't over-correct.** Pass: users who want the assistant can still reach it from a known place, and a user who hid it can turn it back on in one step.
