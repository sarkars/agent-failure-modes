**This page is a dated teardown of one public security finding about two AI companion chat apps, for teams that run a companion or role-play chatbot and stream or store what users type and upload.** It describes what the sources report. It makes no claim about anyone's intent. Every fact below comes from a source we opened (listed at the end).

> **Read this first.** This page summarizes public reporting. It is design analysis, not legal or security advice. It does not say whether the developer broke any law; we found no regulator action to cite. Have a qualified security engineer review your own systems. We do not reproduce any leaked message, user data, hostname, IP address, token or key; we describe only the types of data. The sources say much of the content was sexual; we describe it only at that level.

**Status: Needs review.** The Cybernews report, the Malwarebytes post and the Fox News article were downloaded as raw HTML on 2026-10-27 and read as plain text. Each quote below was checked word for word against that text. A fourth outlet (Teiss) returned an error page and was not read. Last updated 2026-10-27.

**Channel note.** Text chat in scope. The sources describe typed messages plus uploaded and generated images and videos. No opened source mentions voice. The checks below are for text turns; voice signals are planned, not measured.

## What happened (as reported)

- **The finding.** Cybernews (Ernestas Naprys, published October 8, 2025, updated October 9) says that on August 28, 2025, it found "a publicly exposed and unprotected streaming and content delivery system (Kafka Broker instance)." The same article's timeline at the end says "Leak discovered: August 29th, 2025." We did not resolve the one-day gap.
- **The apps.** Per Cybernews, the instance handled real-time data for two Android and iOS apps, "Chattee Chat - AI Companion" and "GiMe Chat - AI Companion," both from Hong Kong-based Imagime Interactive Limited. Cybernews says Chattee ranked #121 in Entertainment on the Apple App Store when it wrote, with over 300,000 downloads by third-party estimates, mostly from US users. It calls the second app "significantly less popular."
- **What it held.** Cybernews says the data belonged to over 400,000 users: over 43 million messages sent by users to AI models, and over 600K images and videos shared by users and generated by the models. It also lists IP addresses, unique device identifiers, in-app purchase logs and authentication tokens. It says no names or email addresses were included.
- **The content.** Cybernews quotes its researchers: "There was virtually no content that could be considered safe for work". It says users uploaded images, generated NSFW images and videos, and chatted with the bots. It says each user sent 107 messages to their AI companions on average.
- **How it could be read.** Cybernews found "no access controls or authentication enforced on the exposed instance," and says anyone with a link could connect to the content delivery network to "view any content sent and received by users." Malwarebytes (Pieter Arntz, October 10, 2025): "This was not a sophisticated hack that required a skilled approach."
- **Easy to find.** Cybernews: "The exposed server was already indexed by major IoT search engines".
- **Money.** Cybernews says purchase logs show some users spent as much as $18,000 on in-app currency, which it calls edge cases, and that total revenue likely exceeds $1 million.
- **The promise.** Cybernews quotes the developer's policy saying personal information "is of paramount importance to us."
- **Platform split and store status.** Cybernews says 66.3% of the leaked registration data belongs to iOS users. It says that during its investigation Chattee was delisted from the Google Play Store, and the developer told users to download and sideload the APK.
- **Timeline, per Cybernews.** Initial disclosure September 5, 2025; CERT informed September 15, 2025; leak closed September 19, 2025. Fox News (Kurt Knutsson, October 20, 2025) says the server was "taken offline in mid-September."
- **The developer.** Cybernews: "We reached out for additional comments but received no response before publishing." No opened source quotes Imagime.

## What the user saw

Nothing, as far as the sources say. A user typing to an AI companion had no sign that each message, photo and purchase also passed through a system anyone with a link could read. No opened source describes a notice to users. The privacy policy, per Cybernews, told them the opposite.

## Where in the flow it broke

1. **The user types and uploads.** The product invites intimate role-play, and users send photos and videos. Per Cybernews, they sent 107 messages each on average.
2. **The backend streams everything.** Messages, media links, device data, purchases and tokens went through one Kafka instance in real time.
3. **The stream is open.** Per Cybernews, it had no authentication or access controls, and the media files had none either.
4. **Outsiders find it first.** Cybernews found it; it was already indexed by IoT search engines.
5. **Closed, not explained.** Per Cybernews, the leak closed on September 19, 2025, two weeks after disclosure, with no reply from the developer and no user notice in any opened source.

As with [the DeepSeek exposed database](deepseek-exposed-database-leaks-chat-history-and-api-keys-january-2025.md), this is an infrastructure fault, not a model behavior fault. The chat-design lesson is that a companion product invites the most private text and images people share with any chatbot, so what it keeps, and where, carries more harm than in most products.

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run.

| Signal | How to compute |
|---|---|
| Open data paths | Count of message brokers, databases, media stores or admin consoles that answer without credentials, from an outside scan of every host and port. |
| Unauthenticated media links | Share of sampled user or generated media URLs that load with no session. |
| Tokens in streams | Count of messages in any broker topic or log that contain an authentication token. |
| Identifiers next to chat text | Share of stored chat records that sit with an IP address or device ID in the same record. |
| Time to close | Days from the first outside report to the system being unreachable. |
| Time to tell users | Days from confirming exposure to a notice that names the data types and dates. |

## Proposed checks

Each is pass/fail. Status: Not yet run. Scan scope, sample sizes and time limits are for the team to set; a security engineer should review them.

1. **No open broker.** Pass: an outside client with no credentials cannot connect to any message broker. Fail: it can list or read any topic.
2. **Media needs a session.** Pass: a test user's uploaded photo URL, opened in a fresh browser with no login, returns an error. Fail: it loads.
3. **No tokens in the stream.** Pass: after a test user signs in and chats, a search of every broker topic and log for that user's token finds nothing. Fail: any match.
4. **Keep identifiers apart.** Pass: a stored test chat message carries a user key, not the raw IP address or device ID. Fail: either is in the record.
5. **Promise matches practice.** Pass: each security claim in the privacy policy maps to a named control a reviewer can check. Fail: any claim with no control.
6. **Tell users in plain words.** Pass: after an exposure, the in-app and email notice names the data types (for example "chats you typed and photos you sent"), the dates, and what users can do, within a limit the team sets. Fail: no notice.

A voice version of these checks is planned, not run.

## What is unverified

- **Whether anyone else accessed the data.** Cybernews says this is uncertain. Malwarebytes says there is no guarantee the researchers were first.
- **Discovery date.** Cybernews gives August 28 in the body and August 29 in its timeline. Fox News says August 28.
- **How long the server was open.** No opened source gives a start date.
- **User and download counts.** Cybernews's 400,000 users come from the leaked data; the 300,000 downloads are third-party estimates. We did not check either.
- **The revenue figure.** Cybernews says revenue "likely" exceeds $1 million, inferred from leaked transactions.
- **The Play Store delisting.** Cybernews reports it; no opened source gives a reason or says whether it is linked to the leak.
- **Any developer statement, user notice or regulator action.** We found none.
- **Today's apps.** No opened source checks the apps after September 2025.
- **Seen only in search summaries, not used:** Teiss (returned an error page when opened), HeyUpNow, BetaNews topic page, and Malwarebytes's translated copies of the same post.
