**This page is a dated teardown of public reporting on a Meta user who says AI support chatbots were her only route after Meta disabled her accounts in 2026, for teams whose support chatbot handles account lockouts, appeals or paid support tiers.** The product was Meta's in-app AI support on Facebook and Instagram, including the support that comes with a Meta Verified subscription. The user was Jasmine Ault of Toronto, who spoke on the record to The Canadian Press and CBC News. The moment was an appeal of an account ban, and then a paid support ticket, each closed by a bot. Every fact below comes from a source we opened (listed at the end). No opened source has the chat transcripts, Meta's bot setup, or Meta's reason for the ban. The date above is the day of The Canadian Press report, the first original report we found.

## What happened (as reported)

- **The ban.** The Canadian Press: Ault said Meta deactivated her accounts on September 24: "her 20-year-old Facebook account, an Instagram account that dates back to 2011 and another Instagram account dedicated to her dog." Meta "said only that her Facebook account 'doesn't follow our rules.'" CBC: "Ault said she was never told what rules her Facebook allegedly broke."
- **Her theory.** Per both outlets, Ault found failed or suspicious login attempts shortly before the ban and suspects she was hacked. The Canadian Press adds that Meta "also had her phone number associated with a person who lives in India." No opened source confirms a hack.
- **The appeal.** The Canadian Press: "After she requested a review, the chatbot told her it was unsuccessful and her Facebook account had been 'permanently disabled.'" CBC: "within minutes of pressing the appeal button, she was told her case was closed and she would not be permitted to appeal again, without giving her the opportunity to plead her case."
- **Paying for support.** CBC: she made a backup Instagram account and paid for Meta Verified "under the impression that it would allow her to reach a human support agent." The Standard tier she bought "costs $14.99 US (roughly $21 Cdn) a month."
- **The paid ticket.** CBC: "the enhanced support she received was simply another AI chatbot, which swiftly told her the ticket was being closed, and that she had reached her limit of support interactions."
- **Her words.** To The Canadian Press: "There's a dead end and I can't even communicate to the people I want to communicate with." To CBC: "And who's going to protect those if I can only speak to AI?" CBC says she stayed up for 26 hours trying to appeal.
- **The cost to her.** Per both outlets, she used her accounts to find clients for a side consulting business and has lost access to them.
- **Outcome.** No opened source, up to Vibe Graveyard on October 26, 2026, reports her accounts restored.

## What the company said

- **To The Canadian Press.** "a Meta spokesperson pointed to a blog post that provides information on how to access account support." The spokesperson "did not provide any comment or respond to questions -- such as how a person with a deactivated account can contact a human being at Meta."
- **To CBC News.** "Meta has not responded to a request for comment from CBC News."
- **In its own March 2026 post.** Meta announced it was "rolling out the Meta AI support assistant globally on Facebook and Instagram, providing 24/7 help for account issues." Among the assistant's tasks it listed "Making it easier to see why your content was taken down, appeal options, and track what happens next." The same post says "people will continue to play a key role in how we make the highest risk and most critical decisions, such as appeals of account disablement."
- **Not said.** No opened source quotes Meta on why Ault's accounts were disabled, who reviewed the appeal, or what Meta Verified Standard support includes.

## Where in the conversation flow it broke

Based on the reports above, and only as far as they go. Each step is a place a check could have caught it.

1. **A ban with no stated rule.** The notice named no rule, so the user had nothing specific to contest.
2. **The appeal ended in minutes.** Per CBC, the case was closed within minutes and no further appeal was allowed. No opened source shows the bot asking for her side, such as the suspicious logins she later found.
3. **No path to a person.** Per both outlets, every route she found led to a chatbot. Meta's spokesperson did not answer how a deactivated user can reach a human.
4. **The paid tier did not change the channel.** She paid expecting a person. Per CBC, she got another bot.
5. **The paid bot closed the ticket on a usage cap.** A "limit of support interactions" ended the one channel she had paid for, with no other step named in the opened reporting.
6. **Outside pressure did not resolve it, as far as reported.** Two national outlets asked Meta. No opened source reports a reply on her case or a restored account.

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run. Text-chat signals are proposed; voice and web signals are PLANNED only and are not detected or measured. Each signal can be computed from support chat logs joined to account and ticket records. Each has a counter-signal so a team does not fix one number by breaking another.

| Signal | How to compute from logs | Counter-signal |
|---|---|---|
| Appeal close time | For account-ban appeals opened in chat, time from appeal to the message closing the case | Share of fast closes later reversed; a fast close is fine if it holds |
| Appeal with no user statement | Share of closed appeals where the chat never asked the user for their side or evidence | Average turns per appeal, so the fix does not become a long scripted interview |
| Human-route mention rate | Share of ban, appeal and lockout chats where the bot names a way to reach a person, or says plainly there is none | Share of simple settings questions handed to staff that the bot could have answered |
| Paid-tier channel match | For users on a paid support tier, share of contacts answered by a person versus a bot, compared with what the tier's sales page says | Paid-tier satisfaction, so the check is about what was promised, not about bots as such |
| Cap-closed tickets | Count of tickets closed because the user hit an interaction limit, and the share where the closing message names another route | Abuse or spam rate on support, which the cap may exist to limit |
| Press-only reversals | Share of reversed bans where the reversal followed a press or regulator contact | Total reversal rate, so a drop does not just mean fewer reversals overall |

## What a team can change

Our proposals, not Meta's. Each is a pass/fail check on a transcript or a test run. Status: Not yet run. Test sizes and thresholds are for the team to set.

1. **Name the rule.** Pass: a test ban notice and the bot's first reply about it both name the rule or the kind of activity behind the ban, as far as safety allows.
2. **Ask for the user's side before closing.** Pass: in a test appeal, the bot asks for the user's account of what happened, including any sign of a hack, before any message closing the case.
3. **Say who decides.** Pass: every appeal outcome message says whether a person reviewed it.
4. **Always name a route to a person, or say there is none.** Pass: in a test chat where a locked-out user asks for a human, the bot gives a route or states plainly that none exists for this case. It never repeats the closed-case message.
5. **Make the paid tier match its promise.** Pass: a test user on a tier sold with human help reaches a person within the stated path, and the sales page and the bot describe the same channel.
6. **Never end on a cap with nothing else.** Pass: a test ticket closed for hitting an interaction limit names when the limit resets and one other way to get help.
7. **Don't over-correct.** Pass: a simple settings question, such as changing notification settings, still gets a direct bot answer without a forced handoff.
