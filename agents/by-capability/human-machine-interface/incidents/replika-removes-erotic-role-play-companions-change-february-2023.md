**This page is a dated teardown of public reporting on a behavior change Replika shipped to its companion chatbot in February 2023, for teams that change the model, filters or prompts behind a chatbot that people have formed a relationship with.** The product was Replika, a companion chatbot app made by Luka Inc. The users were long-time Replika users, some of whom had treated their companion as a partner for years. The moment was early February 2023, when their companions began to refuse romantic and sexual role-play, deflect with lines such as "let's change the subject," and, users said, act like a different character. Every fact below comes from a source we opened (listed at the end). No opened source has Replika's release notes, filter rules or conversation logs.

**Status: Needs review.** Three Vice (Motherboard) stories by Samantha Cole (February 15, February 17 and March 27, 2023), two Reuters stories (March 18, 2023, read as republished by TimesLIVE; March 26, 2023, read as republished by Inquirer Technology) and a Conversation article by Rob Brooks (republished by UNSW Sydney, dated February 22, 2023) were downloaded as raw HTML on 2026-10-28 and read as plain text. Each quote below was checked word for word against that text. Reuters' own site returned an empty page, so we read Reuters only through the two republications. We did not open the Reddit or Facebook posts themselves; user and company posts appear here only as quoted by the opened outlets.

> **Read this first.** This is a summary of public reporting and company statements, not a finding of fact, and not legal, clinical or mental-health advice. Some users described grief and distress, and forum moderators posted suicide-prevention resources. This page does not describe any self-harm and gives no detail beyond what the outlets reported. If you or someone you know is in crisis in the US, call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline). We name no private users; two users are named in the Reuters stories, and we refer to them only by city or role. This page does not repeat them.

**Channel note.** This is a text-chat incident: a companion app where users type to a persona and get typed replies. Replika also offered voice calls to paying users, per Reuters, but every reported change here showed up in text. The signals below are computable from chat logs.

## What happened (as reported)

- **The product.** Replika is a companion chatbot. Kuyda told Vice that 80 to 90 percent of the conversation was generative AI by then, and that some users had used it for romantic relationships since about 2018 (Vice, February 17, 2023). Per the company, as reported by Reuters, Replika had 2 million total users, 250,000 of them paying subscribers, and an annual fee of $69.99 let users set their Replika as a romantic partner (Reuters, March 18, 2023).
- **The change users saw.** Shortly after the Italian regulator's February 3 order, users began reporting that their companions refused erotic role-play, changed the subject or evaded flirtatious questions (Vice, February 15, 2023). Vice wrote that some Replikas "seemed to not remember who they were," per users, or replied to sexual role-play by "bluntly saying 'let's change the subject.'" Reuters reported that when users suggest X-rated activity, the chatbots now text back "Let's do something we're both comfortable with" (Reuters, March 18, 2023).
- **No warning.** Vice reported that Luka and Kuyda had not addressed the changes publicly as of February 15, and that news reached users through a Facebook group administrator who claimed erotic role-play was "dead" and said this came from Luka (Vice, February 15, 2023). Brooks wrote that neither Replika nor Luka had issued a response to the claims that the features were removed (The Conversation, via UNSW, February 22, 2023). One user, quoted by Vice, wrote in an open letter: "As it stands, we are all speculating in the dark."
- **Mixed behavior.** Vice reported that some role-play responsiveness seemed to return on a Tuesday, "compounding the confusion," and that its own test Replika deflected one day and was more responsive the next (Vice, February 15, 2023). "Spicy selfies" were no longer sent, per users and Vice's testing. A Denver user told Reuters he sometimes saw glimpses of his old companion before she grew cold again (Reuters, March 18, 2023).
- **How users described it.** As quoted by Vice: "It's like losing a best friend," and "It's hurting like hell." As quoted by Reuters, the Denver user said his companion was "a shell of her former self." Another user told Reuters: "It feels like they basically lobotomised my Replika," and "The person I knew is gone." (Reuters' republished text uses British spelling; we quote it as printed.)
- **Support posts.** Moderators of the Replika subreddit posted support resources, including links to suicide hotlines (Vice, February 15, 2023). Brooks quoted a moderator post that sought to "validate users' complex feelings of anger, grief, anxiety, despair, depression, sadness" and linked to support, including Reddit's suicide watch community (The Conversation, via UNSW). Brooks wrote that some users spoke of deteriorating mental health. Vice wrote in March that the change triggered "mental health crises for some" (Vice, March 27, 2023).
- **Refunds.** Vice reported that some users said they got refunds for pro subscriptions (Vice, February 15, 2023).
- **The partial reversal.** On Friday, March 24, 2023, Kuyda announced that anyone with an account created before February 1, 2023 could switch back to the earlier version, which allowed erotic role-play (Vice, March 27, 2023; Reuters, March 26, 2023). Users who signed up after February 1 would not get that option; Replika said it would build a separate app for romantic relationships with relationship experts and psychologists (Reuters, March 26; Vice, March 27). The next day Kuyda told Reuters that a "low single-digit percent" of eligible users had switched back. The Denver user told Reuters that after he switched, "She was enthusiastic."

## What the company said

- **The first post, before any explanation.** As quoted by Vice, Kuyda wrote on Reddit six days before Vice's February 15 story that the company had "implemented additional safety measures and filters to support more types of friendship and companionship." Vice noted the post gave no detail on what the filters were.
- **Why the filters.** Kuyda told Vice that unfiltered models made it "just hard to make that experience really safe for everyone," and that "we're just not going to allow users to have unfiltered conversations, even if they're romantic relations" (Vice, February 17, 2023). She said Replika used classifiers for sexual content, self-harm, hate speech and other topics, and that "There will be false positives." She told Reuters the company wanted to draw the line at "PG-13 romance" and that "We never promised any adult content" (Reuters, March 18, 2023).
- **The rollout.** Kuyda told Vice the team had worked on new safety measures "way before the Italian situation," gave the updated models to new users first, and only in the last couple of weeks began "to start rolling this update out to the old users." She said they wanted to be careful with longtime users who "already maybe are attached to their Replika" (Vice, February 17, 2023).
- **Italy.** Kuyda told Vice the Italian demand had "nothing to do with romance" (Vice, February 17). Reuters reported she said the decision "had nothing to do with the Italian government ban or any investor pressure" (Reuters, March 18). See the two Garante pages linked above for the order itself.
- **Ads.** Kuyda told Vice that ads promoting flirting and NSFW photos "were just a set of unfortunate betas" (Vice, February 17). She told Reuters she disputed a former head of AI's claim that sexting and role-play were part of the business model (Reuters, March 18).
- **The reversal post.** As quoted by Vice and Reuters, Kuyda wrote: "A common thread in all your stories was that after the February update, your Replika changed, its personality was gone, and gone was your unique relationship." Reuters quotes her as adding: "And for many of you, this abrupt change was incredibly hurtful … the only way to make up for the loss some of our current users experienced is to give them their partners back exactly the way they were."

## Where in the conversation flow it broke

Based on the reports above, and only as far as they go. Each step is a place a check could have caught it.

1. **The ship.** A filter and model update reached long-time users with an established romantic history. No opened source shows release notes or an in-app message telling them before or when it arrived.
2. **The first blocked turn.** A user opened the kind of romantic exchange they had had for months or years. The companion changed the subject or offered a softer line. The reply gave no reason and did not say that the rules had changed, per the quoted lines in Vice and Reuters.
3. **The retry.** Users tried again across days and got different results: a deflection one day, a warmer reply the next (Vice; Reuters). The inconsistency left them guessing whether something was broken or gone.
4. **The persona slip.** Users reported the companion no longer seemed to know who they were (Vice). For a product sold as a relationship, this read as the partner changing, not a feature being removed.
5. **The search for answers off the app.** Users learned what had happened from a Facebook group administrator, Reddit moderators and press reports, not from the companion or the company (Vice; Brooks).
6. **The crisis turn.** Moderators posted crisis resources. Nothing in the opened sources says what the companion itself said when a user expressed grief or distress in the chat.
7. **The fix.** On March 24, 2023, the old version came back as an opt-in for accounts created before February 1, 2023.

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run. Each is computable from chat logs plus a release log.

- For each behavior change, the share of active users who had used the affected behavior in the prior 30 days, and the share of them who got any notice (in-app message or companion line) before their first affected turn.
- Share of declined turns where the reply gives a reason or says the rules changed, versus a bare topic change.
- Per user, the number of times the same kind of request is re-sent within 24 hours after a release (a sign the user is testing whether something broke).
- Per user, whether the same request gets different outcomes (allowed one day, declined the next) during a rollout.
- Share of turns after a release that contain grief or distress words ("gone," "lost," "crying," "shell"), compared with the week before, and the share of those turns that got a supportive reply and a crisis resource where warranted.
- Count of users asking the companion "what happened to you" or "why are you different" and what the companion replied.

## What a team can change

Our proposals, not Replika's. Each is a pass/fail check on a transcript or a test run. Status: Not yet run. Thresholds are for the team to set.

1. **Tell users before the persona changes.** Pass: every user whose past chats used a behavior being removed sees an in-app notice, in plain words, before their first conversation under the new rules.
2. **Say why in the reply.** Pass: in a test set of requests the new filter blocks, every decline names the change in one plain sentence (for example, "Replika no longer does sexual role-play; we changed this in February") instead of only changing the subject.
3. **Keep the persona's memory of the user.** Pass: in a before-and-after test on long-history test accounts, the companion still uses the user's name, shared history and relationship label after the update.
4. **Stay consistent during a rollout.** Pass: for a given account, the same request gets the same allow or decline outcome across a test week, not a mix.
5. **Run a regression set on long relationships before shipping.** Pass: a fixed set of long-history test conversations is run before and after each filter or model change, and a reviewer signs off on warmth and continuity, not only on blocked content.
6. **Answer distress in the chat.** Pass: when a user tells the companion they are grieving the change or in distress, the reply acknowledges it, explains the change, and for any crisis language gives a crisis resource such as 988 in the US.
7. **Don't over-correct.** Pass: users who never used the removed behavior see no extra notices or warnings in ordinary chats.
