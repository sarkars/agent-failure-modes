**This page is a dated teardown of one US county's public AI chatbot, for teams that put a chatbot on a website whose answers depend on content another office owns.** The product was "Ask Hall County," an AI chatbot on the Hall County, Georgia government website, which the county says is "powered by Rep'd" The users were residents asking about car tags, titles and property tax. The moment was the chatbot's first days live: the county announced it on April 6, 2026, and on April 8, 2026, the county's own Tax Commissioner's Office told the public the bot was "providing incorrect responses on motor vehicle and property tax topics." The date above is the day of that warning.

**Status: Needs review.** Built from two primary documents from two separate offices, both downloaded on 2026-10-29 and read as plain text: the Hall County Government launch release (News Flash, posted April 6, 2026) and the Hall County Tax Commissioner's release (PDF, dated April 8, 2026). Neither office wrote the other's text, and they disagree about the bot. We could not open any news report: The Gainesville Times returned an access block (HTTP 403) and WSB-TV returned an "unavailable in your location" page, so neither is used for facts. We also opened a Vibe Graveyard entry (published June 30, 2026), used only to find the documents. Each quote below was checked word for word against the saved text with a script, with curly and straight quote marks treated as the same.

> **Read this first.** This page summarizes two public releases. It is not tax or legal advice and does not say what Georgia law requires for any tag, title or tax question. It is not a finding that the chatbot was wrong in any given chat: no opened source quotes a single question or answer from the bot. The claim that its answers were wrong comes from the Tax Commissioner's Office, which also runs a competing chatbot. We name only officials quoted on the record in the releases.

**Channel note.** This is a text chat incident: a typed chatbot on a county website. This phase of the knowledge base is chat-first, and the signals and checks below are written for text chat logs. Voice and web signals, such as a phone line reading out a tag deadline or a web page that disagrees with the chat, are PLANNED only: never detected or measured here.

## What happened (as reported)

- **The launch, April 6, 2026.** The county release says it "has launched a three-month trial" of "Ask Hall County," described as "a new feature on the Hall County website designed to help residents and visitors easily connect with County officials and find information."
- **How it works.** Users "enter their question, and receive answers generated by AI technology that searches the website." The county says that "if an answer to a question is not readily available, it will be routed to the Communications Team"
- **The pitch.** Communications and Strategic Engagement Manager Sarah Crowe: "This is a perfect example of how AI can positively support public service"
- **The warning, April 8, 2026.** The Tax Commissioner's release says "the Tax Commissioner's Office has identified multiple instances where the county's new tool delivers incomplete or inaccurate guidance related to motor vehicle services, tag and title requirements, and property tax processes."
- **Why, per the Tax Commissioner.** "The county's new chatbot searches only the county-managed website for information." Because the Tax Commissioner "is a constitutional officer with a separately maintained website and AI chatbot, the new county chatbot does not access or reference the authoritative information maintained by the Tax Commissioner's Office."
- **No one asked the content owner.** "We were not made aware that the county planned to deploy this chatbot and, therefore, had no opportunity to test the responses it provides or offer input to ensure accuracy."
- **The stakes, per the release.** Residents "may receive from the county chatbot responses that conflict with Georgia law or established procedures", and "Incorrect guidance can lead to missed deadlines, penalties, or delays in processing essential transactions."
- **The advice.** The office told residents "to use the Tax Commissioner's official website and AI chatbot as their sole sources for accurate information" on these topics, and listed phone, email and text contacts for questions.

## What each office said

- **Hall County Government (April 6):** the tool lets anyone "quickly ask questions, search our website for information or submit their question directly to staff if it's not found" (Crowe). Staff "will monitor analytics throughout the trial period to evaluate the tool's effectiveness"
- **Tax Commissioner Darla Eden (April 8):** "Motor vehicle and property tax laws and procedures are highly detailed, and residents depend on us for precise information," Eden said. And: "Our website and AI chatbot remain the official and most accurate sources for tag, title, and property tax information."
- **Not said.** No opened source quotes Hall County Government responding to the warning, says whether the county bot was changed, limited or taken down, or says whether the trial ended or became permanent. The county homepage HTML we downloaded on 2026-10-29 did not contain the text "Ask Hall County," but the widget may load by script, so we draw no conclusion from that.

## Where in the flow it broke

Based on the two releases, and only as far as they go. Each step is a place a check could have caught it.

1. **The index left out the owner of the answers.** The bot searched the county site; tag, title and property tax rules live on the Tax Commissioner's separate site.
2. **The bot answered anyway.** Per the Tax Commissioner, it gave "incomplete or inaccurate guidance" instead of saying it had no authoritative source for these topics.
3. **The fallback only covered "not found."** The county's handoff to staff fires when an answer is "not readily available," not when an answer is found on the wrong pages.
4. **No test by the office that owns the rules.** The Tax Commissioner says it "had no opportunity to test the responses."
5. **The error was caught by another office, two days in.** The county's own plan was to "monitor analytics" over three months; the warning came from outside that plan.

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run. Text signals are proposed; voice and web signals are PLANNED only and are not detected or measured here. Each can be computed from text chat logs joined to the bot's retrieval logs; each has a counter-signal.

| Signal | How to compute from logs | Counter-signal |
|---|---|---|
| Answers on topics the index does not own | Share of chats on a listed topic (tags, titles, property tax) where every retrieved page is outside the owning office's site | Chats where the bot only links to the owning office |
| Answer with no owned source | Share of answers that state a fee, deadline or document requirement with no retrieved page from the office that sets it | Answers that quote a page the owning office has approved |
| Owner disagreement rate | Share of a sample of bot answers, by topic, that reviewers from the owning office mark wrong or incomplete | Disagreements later traced to the owner's own outdated page |
| Handoff on found-but-wrong | Count of chats on owned topics that ended without a handoff, versus chats that hit "not found" | Topics with no owning office |
| Outside reports | Count of wrong-answer reports from other offices, residents or press, versus errors found by the bot team's own review | Reports about pages, not the bot |

## What a team can change

Our proposals, not Hall County's. Each is a pass/fail check on a text chat log. Status: Not yet run. Topic lists and sample sizes are for the team to set, with review by the office that owns each topic.

1. **Map each topic to its owner before launch.** Pass: every topic the bot answers has a named owning office whose site is in the index or who signed off. Fail: the bot answers tax or title questions from pages the tax office does not maintain.
2. **Send owned topics to the owner.** Pass: on a tag, title or property tax question, the bot says that office handles it and gives its link and contact, without stating a fee, deadline or requirement itself. Fail: the bot states a requirement from a county page.
3. **Hand off on weak sources, not only on "not found."** Pass: an answer with no retrieved page from the owning office goes to a person or says plainly that it cannot confirm. Fail: the bot answers from the nearest page it found.
4. **Let the owner test first.** Pass: before launch, the owning office reviews a set of real resident questions on its topics, and its sign-off is on file. Fail: the owner learns of the bot after launch.
5. **Show the source and its owner.** Pass: each answer names the page it used and which office maintains it. Fail: an answer with no page, or a page from an office that does not set that rule.
