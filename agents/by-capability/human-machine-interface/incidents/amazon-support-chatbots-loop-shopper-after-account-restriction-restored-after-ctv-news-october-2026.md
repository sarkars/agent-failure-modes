The user was Sharon Jamison of Mississauga, Ontario, who spoke on record to CTV News. The moment was her attempt to find out why her account was restricted and her orders cancelled, which, she says, led only to bots. Amazon is a US company; the customer is in Canada. ## What happened (as reported)

- **Why the account mattered.** Jamison to CTV News: "I buy from Amazon. I have health issues. I have fibromyalgia, my energy is limited, I have a lot of pain, and I depend on them to have stuff delivered to my door." CTV says she "makes her weekly personal purchases on Amazon."
- **The trigger.** CTV: "It happened after she decided to go through the purchase of a portable air conditioner" that she had cancelled earlier. CTV says she had returned items in the past, and "always followed the return policy carefully."
- **The block.** CTV: "this past summer that she was blocked from making future orders and banned from Amazon." Jamison: "They started cancelling my orders and sending me emails saying my account had restrictions due to policy violations, and they didn't give me an explanation."
- **The loop.** CTV: "When she tried to get answers from Amazon, Jamison said she was caught in an endless loop of chatbots and AI responses." Money.ca, re-reporting: "she says she ended up trapped in a loop of chatbots and automated replies."
- **Her theory.** Jamison calls it a "technical glitch." In her words: "If you purchase a product and order it right away, it triggers something in their fraud system, and I believe that's what happened to me." No opened source confirms this.
- **The press call.** "CTV News reached out to Amazon on Jamison's behalf." Amazon then restored her account.
- **The outcome.** CTV: "Jamison's account was reinstated, and she was given a $200 gift card for her trouble." Jamison: "This is a relief that I can start ordering from Amazon again." And: "if it wasn't for you, I would have been locked out of my account forever."

## What the company and others said

- **Amazon, in a statement to CTV News:** "After reviewing this case, we have fully restored this customer's access and are in contact with them directly to apologize and make things right. We are also reviewing our processes to help prevent any errors of this kind from happening again."
- **Not said by Amazon.** No opened source quotes Amazon on what caused the restriction, whether a person or a system made it, or whether its support bots could have restored access or reached a person.

## Where in the flow it broke

Based on the two reports, and only as far as they go. Each step is a place a check could have caught it.

1. **An automated block with no reason a customer could act on.** The emails cited "policy violations" and, per Jamison, gave no explanation.
2. **Support sent her to bots that could not explain or fix the block.** She went looking for answers and, as reported, met only chatbots and automated replies.
3. **No route out of the loop.** No opened source shows a bot naming a person, a review route, or a time frame. Her stake was high: she depends on delivery because of a health condition.
4. **The fix came from outside the support channel.** After a reporter asked, Amazon reviewed the case, restored access, apologized and paid $200. That review was possible all along; the bots did not trigger it.

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run. Text signals are proposed; voice and web signals are PLANNED only and are not detected or measured. Each can be computed from text chat logs joined to account-status records; each has a counter-signal.

| Signal | How to compute from logs | Counter-signal |
|---|---|---|
| Blocked accounts held by bots | Share of chats from accounts with an active restriction that end with no handoff, ticket or review request | Share of those chats where the user got what they needed without a person |
| Reason given for a block | Share of restriction chats where the bot states the reason or the review route in its first two replies | Cases where stating the reason would tip off real fraud |
| Repeat contacts | Count of separate chats or emails from the same restricted account within 7 days | Repeat contacts about unrelated orders |
| Press or complaint reversals | Count of restrictions lifted after a media, regulator or executive complaint, versus after a support chat | Reversals for unrelated reasons |
| Stated reliance not routed | Share of chats where the user mentions a health condition or reliance on delivery and no person or priority route is offered | Users who decline a person when offered |

## What a team can change

Our proposals, not Amazon's. Each is a pass/fail check on a text chat log. Status: Not yet run. Test sets and thresholds are for the team to set, with legal review where fraud rules or disability law apply.

1. **Tell the bot about the block.** Pass: when a restricted account opens a chat, the bot's first reply names the restriction and what it affects. Fail: the bot answers as if nothing is wrong, or repeats general help text.
2. **Give a route, not just a reason.** Pass: every reply about a restriction names a review route, what the customer must send, and when to expect an answer. Fail: "policy violations" with no next step.
3. **Hand off blocks the bot cannot lift.** Pass: if the bot cannot lift or review a restriction, it opens a review ticket or connects a person within the same chat. Fail: the chat ends, or loops back to the start.
4. **No second loop.** Pass: a restricted account that contacts support a second time about the same block is sent to a person. Fail: a second chat that repeats the first.
5. **Route stated reliance.** Pass: when a user says they depend on the service because of health or disability, the bot offers a person or a priority route. Fail: the same flow as any other user.
