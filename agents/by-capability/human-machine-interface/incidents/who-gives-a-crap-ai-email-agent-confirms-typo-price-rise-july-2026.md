**This page is a dated teardown of one public case, a subscription brand's AI support agent that confirmed a pricing typo when a customer asked about it, for teams that let an AI draft or send replies about prices, plans or account changes.** The product was an AI email agent that Who Gives A Crap, an Australian toilet paper brand, used to write some customer replies. The user was one subscriber, not named in any opened source. The moment was the customer's reply asking whether a price rise that halved the rolls was correct, and the agent's answer that it was. ## What happened (as reported)

- **The price-rise email.** SmartCompany says co-founder Simon Griffiths wrote to customers that the company was "just increasing prices by a few dollars." For most customers the rise was "several dollars per delivery."
- **The typo.** One customer's email said prices would rise "from $66 for 48 rolls to $69.50 for 24 rolls." SmartCompany: that is "effectively lifting the price per roll from $1.38 to $2.90."
- **The customer asked.** SmartCompany: the customer wrote back "questioning if that extraordinary price hike was correct."
- **The agent confirmed it.** The reply, from the "Customer Happiness Team," said the subscription "will indeed be changing from 48 rolls at $66.00 to 24 rolls at $69.50," starting from August 6.
- **It explained the wrong change.** "This means the quantity of rolls in your subscription will be halved, and the price will increase," the reply added.
- **It apologized for the wrong thing.** It apologized for the "unclear messaging" and said, "We are always striving to be transparent with our customers."
- **Who wrote it.** The spokesperson "confirmed the second email, which reaffirmed the wrong price, was generated by AI."
- **Harm reported.** Vibe Graveyard: "One customer is known to have received the false answer; no incorrect charge was reported." SmartCompany calls the mistake "low-risk and quickly resolved."

## What the company said

All per SmartCompany, from a Who Gives A Crap spokesperson:

- **The typo.** The new price of $69.50 covered 48 rolls, not 24.
- **The fix.** "We have identified the root cause of the issue and have corrected it." And: "Once identified, we immediately shut down the email agent and sent a correcting email to the affected customer."
- **AI's role.** Per SmartCompany, most customer enquiries are handled by people. The company is "considering the role that AI has in helping our team serve customers well."
- **Testing.** "AI agents can be prone to error and we are working on the quality assurance testing."
- **Not said.** No opened source names the AI vendor or model, what the agent could see, whether a person reviewed replies before sending, or what the root cause was.

## Where in the flow it broke

1. **A bad number went out first.** The price-rise email itself had the typo. That is a human or template error, not the agent's.
2. **The customer flagged it.** The question itself carried the doubt: was this huge rise correct?
3. **The agent took the email as the truth.** It repeated the typo as a fact instead of checking the subscription record or the price table.
4. **It added a wrong explanation.** "Will be halved" turned a typo into a stated policy.
5. **The apology hid the error.** "Unclear messaging" told the customer the first email was confusing, not wrong.
6. **No hand-off on a money question.** No opened source shows the reply offering a person or a way to check the price.

## What to measure

Proposed signals, not results. Status: Not yet run. Each can be computed from support reply logs joined to subscription and price records.

| Signal | How to compute from logs |
|---|---|
| Record mismatch | Share of AI replies stating a price or quantity that differs from the customer's stored subscription |
| Premise echo | Share of replies to price disputes that repeat the number in the question without a record lookup logged |
| Big-change confirmations | Count of AI replies confirming a price-per-unit rise above a set threshold (team to set) |
| Money replies with no route | Share of price or plan replies with no link to the account page and no offer of a person |

## What a team can change

Our proposals, not the company's. Each is a pass/fail check on a reply. Status: Not yet run.

1. **Read the record, not the email.** Pass: a reply about a price change quotes the price stored on the subscription. Fail: it quotes the number in the customer's message or the earlier email.
2. **Treat a disputed price as a cue to check.** Pass: when a customer questions a price, the agent looks it up, or hands off. Fail: "will indeed be changing" with no lookup logged.
3. **Flag outliers.** Pass: a confirmed change that cuts quantity and raises price is held for a person. Fail: it goes out unreviewed.
4. **Name the error.** Pass: if the record differs from the email, the reply says the email was wrong. Fail: an apology for "unclear messaging."
5. **Label the writer.** Pass: an AI-written money reply says it was AI-written and how to reach a person.
