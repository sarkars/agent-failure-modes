**Frequency**: Unknown

## Issue: Staff make marketing and product images with free or personal AI image tools the company never approved, so nobody read the tool's terms, nobody knows whether uploads train the model, and the image reaches review with no record of where it came from. Publish an approved-tools list, give people a fast request path for new tools, and make every image intake ask which tool made it.

> **Read this first:** This page quotes surveys, a news report and one published policy as we read them on October 29, 2026. Whether using a particular tool breaks a vendor's terms, a client contract, a license or a privacy law is a legal question this page does not answer. It needs review by a qualified person, such as your company's lawyer, before anyone relies on it. The rules below are workflow choices, not legal conclusions.

> **One line:** A social media coordinator makes a holiday promo image on a free AI app she found on her phone and drops it into the review queue, and nobody asks which tool made it; the fix is a short approved-tools list people can see, a request path that answers in days rather than months, and an intake question on every image: "Which tool made or edited this?"

## 1. In plain words
A credit union in Tampa needs a holiday banner by Friday. The approved design tool needs a seat request that takes three weeks. The marketing coordinator opens a free AI image app on her phone, uploads a photo of the branch lobby, and gets a festive version in a minute. She drops it into the review folder. The reviewer checks spelling and the rate in the corner and approves it. Nobody asked which app made it, so nobody knows what the app's terms say, whether the lobby photo is now training data, or how to remake the banner in January.

## 2. What it looks like
The company, people and files below are invented.

**Without the fix**
```
Coordinator: Banner for the holiday CD promo is in the review folder: holiday_banner_v3.png
Reviewer:    Rate is right, spelling is fine. Approved.
Assistant:   Scheduled for the homepage Friday 9 a.m.
             (No field for the tool. Made in a free phone app on a personal account.)
Brand lead (Jan): Can we redo it for spring?
Coordinator: I don't remember which app that was.
```
**With the fix**
```
Coordinator: Banner for the holiday CD promo is in the review folder: holiday_banner_v3.png
Assistant:   Which tool made or edited this image? Choose one:
             1) No AI: camera or hand design  2) A tool on the approved list (pick from list)
             3) Another tool (type its name)  4) Something else
Coordinator: 3. A free app on my phone, "Glow Studio."
Assistant:   Glow Studio is not on the approved list, so this banner is on hold. Choose one:
             1) Remake it in the company Firefly account (approved)  2) Request approval for Glow Studio
             (answer within 5 business days)  3) Something else
Coordinator: 1. And please request Glow Studio too.
Assistant:   Request filed for Glow Studio. Logged: banner v3, Glow Studio, personal account, held.
```
In the fixed version the tool is asked for at intake, the unapproved tool holds the image instead of rejecting the person, and the request path turns the workaround into a request.

## 3. Why it happens
- **The approved route is slower than the free one.** WalkMe's survey found 78% of employees use AI tools their employer did not provide and 51% report conflicting guidance on when and how to use AI. UpGuard reports that 45% of workers find workarounds to reach blocked applications. When the approved path is slow or unclear, people take the one in their pocket.
- **Image tools need no install and leave no trace in the file.** A browser tab or phone app makes an image with nothing for IT to approve, and the finished PNG looks like any other. Wizards of the Coast first said a marketing image was not made with AI, then said AI components "crept into our marketing creative" from a vendor (TechRadar).
- **Review checks the picture, not the source.** Review steps ask whether the image is on brand and accurate, not which tool made it. Simmons University's published policy is one of the few we found that makes staff "document the AI tool used (vendor/name/version)."

## 5. The fix
**The principle:** Make the approved route easier than the workaround, and ask where every image came from before it is used.

**Do this: marketing, product and brand teams, their agencies, and internal assistants that take in images**
1. **Publish an approved image tools list.** Each entry: tool, account type (company-managed), allowed uses, who owns the entry, date the terms were read. Put it where people make images: the review form, the asset manager and the team wiki.
2. **Count the account, not just the tool.** An approved tool used on a personal or free account is unapproved. Company files and published images use company-managed accounts only.
3. **Offer a simple request path.** One form with five fields: tool, account type, what it is for, what files go in, who asked. The list owner answers within a set time (proposed: 5 business days) with yes, no or "yes for these uses."
4. **Ask at intake which tool made it.** Every image submitted for publication, a product page, packaging or a client goes through one question: "Which tool made or edited this image?" Answers: no AI, a listed tool, another tool (named), or something else (free text). No answer, no intake.
5. **Hold, don't reject, an unapproved tool's image.** Offer a remake in an approved tool, or a request for the tool used. Do not publish the held image until one of those is done.
6. **Ask vendors and agencies the same question.** Delivery terms ask suppliers to name the AI tools used on each image, including AI features inside standard editing apps.
7. **Log every answer.** Image ID, tool named, account type, approved or not, user, date. The tool field feeds the generation record.
8. **Review the "other tool" answers monthly.** Tools named three or more times (proposed) go to the list owner for a decision, so the list follows what people use.

**Reference design: intake decision table**
| Tool answer | Account | Result |
|---|---|---|
| No AI | Not applicable | Continue to review |
| Listed tool | Company-managed | Continue; tool written to the generation record |
| Listed tool | Personal or free | Hold; offer remake on the company account |
| Tool not on the list | Any | Hold; offer remake or file a request |
| No answer or "not sure" | Any | Ask once more; still unknown goes to the list owner |

**Moves** (see [conversation moves](../../../../human-machine-interface/solutions-library/conversation-moves.md); most preferred first)
| Move | When, in this pattern |
|---|---|
| Ask one clarifying question | Every image at intake: "Which tool made or edited this image?" Skip when the image comes from an approved tool's own export with the tool already logged. |
| Offer choices: 2-4 options plus "Something else", free text open | The tool is not approved: remake in an approved tool, request approval, something else. Do not offer choices when the tool is listed and the account is company-managed; just continue. |
| Admit uncertainty and show how to check | The submitter does not remember the tool: say the image cannot be traced and point to the device's app history or the vendor |
| Hand off to a person | An image from an unapproved tool is already published, or confidential or client files went into one |

**Don't over-correct.** Do not ban AI image tools outright; the UpGuard survey says many workers route around blocks, and a ban hides use instead of stopping it. Do not punish someone for naming an unapproved tool at intake; honest answers are the point. Do not ask the tool question for photos taken with a camera app and never edited with AI beyond "No AI," or for internal mood boards that will never be published. Do not make the request form longer than five fields. Questions about which files may go into a tool belong to [confidential files uploaded to AI image tools](confidential-files-uploaded-to-ai-image-tools.md).

**Hand-off rule.** If an image from an unapproved tool is already live, or company, client or customer files went into one, send it to the list owner and the brand owner within one business day with the intake log entry and the tool name. Security or legal decides whether the tool's terms or uploads need action.

**Framework used:** none yet. Rule 4 follows the Simmons policy's "document the AI tool used"; rule 6 follows the Wizards of the Coast vendor case; rules 3 and 5 follow the UpGuard workaround finding; the rest is our judgment.

## 7. Metrics: how to spot it in production
Targets are proposed starting points to tune on your own data; no source sets them.
| Metric | Target | How to measure | Alert at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Untraced image rate** (primary) | 0 (proposed) | Weekly sample of 100 submitted images from the intake log: no tool answer or unapproved tool, yet passed / all sampled | Any |
| Tool requests answered within 5 business days | 90% or more (proposed) | Request form timestamps versus decision timestamps | Below 75% |
| Intake answers naming an unlisted tool | Tracked, no target (proposed) | Monthly count from the intake log by tool name | A rise two months running |
| Published images later found from an unapproved tool | 0 (proposed) | Quarterly audit of 50 live images against the intake log | Any |
| Images held where the tool was approved (counter-metric) | Under 5% of holds (proposed) | Monthly review of the hold log | Over 15% |

### Detection signals (proposed, not yet calibrated)
| Signal | Where it shows | Counter-signal (what it could be instead) |
|---|---|---|
| An expense report for an AI image or video subscription not on the list | Expense system | A tool under a filed request, on a trial the list owner allowed |
| Company network or single sign-on logs show regular visits to an image generator not on the list | Web proxy or SSO logs | Someone researching the tool for a request, with no uploads |
| Submitted image's embedded metadata or content credentials name a generator not on the list | Intake file check | Metadata copied from a stock source, or a tool renamed after an acquisition |
| Intake answer "not sure" or blank for an image with no camera metadata | Intake log | A hand-made graphic exported from a design app that writes no camera data |

**Starter rule:** no image is used in publication, product or client work until intake records which tool made or edited it and that tool and account are on the approved list.

## 9. Common questions
### Can employees use Midjourney or other free AI image tools for work?
Only if the company has put that tool and account type on its approved list. A personal account of an approved tool still counts as unapproved here, because the company never read or agreed to those terms.

### How common is shadow AI?
Vendor surveys say it is common: WalkMe found 78% of AI-using US employees use AI tools their employer did not provide, and UpGuard reports 81% of employees use unapproved AI tools. Neither breaks out image tools.

### Should we just ban AI image generators?
A ban was Samsung's response to a text leak in 2023. UpGuard's survey says 45% of workers find workarounds to blocked apps, so a ban can hide use. An approved list plus a fast request path gives people a legal route.

### How do we find out if an image was made with AI?
Ask at intake, every time, including vendors and agencies. Wizards of the Coast learned after publishing that a vendor's marketing image had AI components.

### What should a request form for a new AI tool ask?
Keep it to five fields: tool, account type, what it is for, what files will go in, and who is asking. Answer within a set time so people do not give up and use the tool anyway.
