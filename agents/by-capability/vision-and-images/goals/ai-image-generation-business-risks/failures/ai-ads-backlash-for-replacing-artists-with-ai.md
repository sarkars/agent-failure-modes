**Frequency**: Occasional

## Issue: A brand runs an AI-made ad, or AI models, where people expect artists, actors, models or photographers, and the public reads it as "they fired the artists" whatever the pixels look like. Before launch, record which human roles AI took over and who still did the work, test the campaign against that objection, label it so people can see it, and have a named owner ready to answer or pull it.

> **Read this first:** Statements on this page about jobs, labor groups and proposed laws repeat what the cited news reports say. This page does not say what any law, union agreement or talent contract requires, and it needs review by a qualified person before anyone relies on it. One incident (McDonald's) is in the Netherlands, not the US, and the AI models in the Guess ad were made by a London agency for US Vogue. The fix below is a brand and workflow choice, not a legal conclusion.

> **One line:** A marketing team uses an AI video tool for its holiday spot, or AI models for its catalog, judges it only on how it looks and how it tests, and launches it with a small disclaimer or none, so the first public story is "the brand replaced its artists," the brand's answer arrives late and vague, and sometimes the ad comes down; the fix is a short replacement review before launch: which human roles AI took over, who still did the work, a stated reason that is not "more diverse people," a visible label, a pre-written answer, and a named owner who decides fast.

## 1. In plain words
A brand has a holiday ad people love. This year the team remakes it with an AI video tool. It looks fine and it tests well. Then it airs, and the first posts are not about the trucks or the snow. They say the company stopped paying artists. The brand's reply comes days later and says something about "human creativity and technology." Nobody had written down who actually worked on the ad, so there was nothing concrete to say.

## 2. What it looks like
The brand, people and tool below are invented. The objections follow the reactions reported in section 4.

**Without the fix**
```
Brand lead:  Make our "Home for the Holidays" spot with the AI video tool. Same story as 2019, 30 seconds.
Ad tool:     Done. 30-second film, 14 shots, generated characters and voiceover. Ready to export.
Brand lead:  Pre-test scored above our benchmark. Ship it Monday on YouTube and TV.
             (Monday, replies under the post)
Viewer:      "So you fired the animators who made the 2019 one?"
Social team: "We use the best of human creativity and technology."   (No one knows who worked on it.)
             (Wednesday: the brand pulls the ad.)
```
**With the fix**
```
Brand lead:  Make our "Home for the Holidays" spot with the AI video tool. Same story as 2019, 30 seconds.
Ad tool:     Before export: the 2019 spot credited 2 actors, a voice actor and an animation studio.
             This version generates all of them. Replacement review needed. Choose one:
             1) Keep the voice actor and studio, AI for backgrounds  2) Full AI, fill in the review  3) Something else
Brand lead:  2. Owner is D. Price.
Ad tool:     Review saved: roles AI took over (actors, voice, animation), 11 named crew credited,
             on-screen label "Made with AI by our creative team," answer drafted. Owner: D. Price.
```
In the fixed version the tool names the human roles AI is taking over, offers to keep some, and nothing ships without credits, a visible label, an answer and an owner.

## 3. Why it happens
- **The review checks the picture, not the people.** Teams judge an AI ad on looks and pre-tests. Coca-Cola's AI remake drew an "overwhelmingly positive" response and a 5.9 score in System1 testing (Adweek), and still turned into a backlash about artists. Pre-tests ask "do you like it?", not "who did they stop paying?"
- **AI is pitched as solving a people problem.** Levi's AI models were meant to show shoppers "different types of people" (NBC News, paraphrasing the project). Critics said it was a cheap way to address representation. Using AI faces for diversity puts the brand's motive, not the image, on trial.
- **There is no record of who did the work.** Toys "R" Us's agency said about a dozen people worked on its Sora film and that it did not replace workers, and McDonald's production partner posted "Ten people, five weeks, full-time" (NBC News). Those answers came after the criticism, from the agency, in news reports and a post since deleted. With no record and no prepared answer at launch, the brand's first word is vague or late.

## 5. The fix
**The principle:** Before an AI campaign launches, the brand knows which human work AI took over, who still did the work, and what it will say when someone asks.

**Do this: brands, agencies and AI creative tools**
1. **Trigger a replacement review.** Any campaign where AI generates people (models, actors, voices) or artwork a human used to make for this brand needs a review before export or launch. The tool flags it; it does not wait to be asked.
2. **Map the roles AI took over.** List each human role in the previous comparable campaign or brief (actor, model, voice, animator, photographer, illustrator) and mark it kept, reduced or replaced by AI.
3. **Offer a mixed option.** When roles are replaced, the tool offers at least one version that keeps some people (for example the voice actor or the photographer) before full AI is chosen.
4. **Credit the people who did the work.** Record named crew, agency and studio with roles. This list is the basis of any public answer; do not release a headcount you cannot back with names.
5. **Do not use AI faces to answer a diversity goal.** If the reason given is showing more kinds of people, cast real people. Levi's critics read this as a cheap way to address representation (section 4).
6. **Label so a viewer sees it.** An on-screen or in-image label in normal type, for the full length of a video, not only a small disclaimer. Wording follows the brand's written policy.
7. **Test the objection, not only the ad.** Pre-launch review asks a small panel, including people from the affected crafts, "Who do you think lost work here?" A high pre-test score alone does not clear launch.
8. **Draft the answer before launch.** A short statement naming what AI did, what people did (from rule 4) and why. Do not claim "no jobs were replaced" unless the role map in rule 2 shows that.
9. **Name an owner and a pull rule.** One named person decides within one business day whether to keep, change or pull the campaign once replacement complaints appear (section 7 signals).

**Reference design: replacement review (one per campaign)**
```yaml
campaign_id: HOLIDAY-2026-TV
ai_generates: [actors, voiceover, animation]
previous_roles: {actors: 2, voice_actor: 1, animation_studio: 1}
role_map: {actors: replaced, voice_actor: replaced, animation_studio: reduced}
mixed_option_offered: true
human_credits: [{name: "R. Alvarez", role: director}, {name: "Northline Studio", role: compositing}]
reason_stated: "Faster seasonal versions; creative team directed every shot"
diversity_goal_met_by_ai_faces: false
label: {text: "Made with AI by our creative team", placement: on_screen_full_length}
objection_test: {panel_size: 8, includes_crafts: true, top_answer: "animators"}
prepared_answer: on_file
owner: D. Price
pull_rule: decide_within_1_business_day
```

**Moves** (see [conversation moves](../../../../human-machine-interface/solutions-library/conversation-moves.md); most preferred first)
| Move | When, in this pattern |
|---|---|
| Offer choices: 2-4 options plus "Something else", free text open | The tool finds replaced roles: keep some people, full AI with review, something else |
| Ask one clarifying question | The brief has no previous campaign: "Who would normally have made this: actors, a photographer, an illustrator?" |
| Admit uncertainty and show how to check | The tool cannot tell which roles a past campaign used: say so and ask for its credits |
| Hand off to a person | Replacement complaints appear after launch, or the stated reason is a diversity goal |

**Don't over-correct.** AI in a campaign is not banned. AI used for backgrounds, cleanup, storyboards or extra sizes of a real shoot does not need a replacement review when no person, performer or artwork role changed. Do not pull an ad on the first angry post; the owner weighs the signals. Do not overstate human work to calm critics; the answer uses only what rule 4 recorded. Do not slap a "Made with AI" label on a real photo shoot.

**Hand-off rule.** Once replacement complaints appear (section 7), the owner gets the replacement review, the prepared answer and a sample of the posts, and decides keep, change or pull within one business day. Any public answer goes out only from the prepared answer, updated by the owner.

**Framework used:** none yet. Rules 6 and 7 answer the Coca-Cola case (small disclaimer, positive pre-test); rule 5 answers Levi's; rules 4 and 8 answer Toys "R" Us and McDonald's; the rest is our judgment.

### Detection signals (proposed, not yet calibrated)
| Signal | Where it shows | Counter-signal (what it could be instead) |
|---|---|---|
| Replies or posts say "fired the artists," "real models," "out of work" or "soulless" | Comments under the ad, brand mentions | A general debate about AI not tied to this ad |
| Creators or craft groups (actors, models, animators) post about the campaign | Social listening by account type | Praise from the same groups |
| The brand's answer is a slogan with no named people | Published statement vs. review record | A short first reply that links to the full credits |
| The ad is pulled or edited within days of launch | Ad platform change log | A planned flight end date |
| Critics fault the concept, not the AI | Sampled comments | Both at once: McDonald's critics faulted the AI and the concept |

**Starter rule:** no campaign that uses AI in place of people or artwork launches without a role map, named credits, a visible label, a prepared answer and a named owner.

## 9. Common questions
### Why did people hate the Coca-Cola AI Christmas ad?
Reports cite two things: it looked "uncanny" to many viewers, and some designers said Coca-Cola was saving money by not hiring artists. It still tested well before the backlash.

### Did the Toys "R" Us Sora ad replace artists?
Its agency said no, and that about a dozen people worked on it for about three months. Critics still said the company "fired our artists." The answer came after the criticism, not with the launch.

### Why was the McDonald's AI Christmas ad pulled?
McDonald's Netherlands removed it after feedback. Critics attacked both the AI and the idea of calling Christmas "the most terrible time of the year."

### Should my brand use AI models instead of real models?
That is a brand decision. If you do, record which jobs it replaces, credit the people who still worked on it, label it, and do not present AI faces as your diversity effort.

### How do we announce an AI-made ad without backlash?
No opened source shows a way to avoid it fully. Say plainly what AI did and who did the rest, using names you can back up, and have one person ready to decide fast.
