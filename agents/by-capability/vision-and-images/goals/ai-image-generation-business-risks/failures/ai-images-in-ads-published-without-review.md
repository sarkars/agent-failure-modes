**Frequency**: Common

## Issue: Ads, listings and newsletters go out with AI-made or AI-altered images that no person checked, sometimes because an ad platform's AI tool edited the approved photo. Require a named sign-off on the exact version that runs, keep platform AI edits off by default, tag every asset's origin and compare the live ad with the approved file.

> **Read this first:** This page describes platform labeling features and company statements as the cited sources report them. It does not say what any law requires about disclosing AI in ads. That question needs review by a qualified person, such as an advertising lawyer, before anyone relies on it. The disclosure rules below are product and brand choices, not legal conclusions.

> **One line:** A retailer approves a vendor's real product photo, but the version that runs is one an ad platform's AI tool changed, or one a vendor quietly made with AI, and customers spot the extra handlebars or the odd dragon teeth before anyone at the company does; the fix is that no image runs without a named person approving the exact version that runs, platform AI enhancements stay off unless a person approves each variant, every asset records whether AI made or changed it, and the live ad is checked against the approved file after launch.

## 1. In plain words
A store gets a real photo from a bike maker's shoot and runs it as an Instagram ad. The ad platform has an AI tool switched on that "personalizes" the image. The bike in the live ad now has a second set of handlebars. Nobody at the store looked at the live ad, so riders on Reddit find it first. In other cases the AI came in earlier: a vendor or a stock site supplied AI art, and the brand said "no AI was used" before it checked.

## 2. What it looks like
The store, vendor and people below are invented. The settings are the ones REI's statement and an agency blog describe (section 4).

**Without the fix**
```
Coordinator: Launch the spring road bike promo on Instagram with the vendor photo. Same setup as March.
Ad tool:     Campaign duplicated and published. Creative enhancements: recommended settings applied.
Coordinator: Great, thanks.
             (Six days later, a comment on the ad)
Shopper:     "Why does this bike have handlebars on the seat? Did AI make this?"
Social team: "No AI was used in this image."   (Nobody has checked the live ad yet.)
```
**With the fix**
```
Coordinator: Launch the spring road bike promo on Instagram with the vendor photo. Same setup as March.
Ad tool:     Before I publish: duplicating turned 3 AI enhancements back on (image expansion,
             background generation, text variations). Your account default is off. Choose one:
             1) Keep all off  2) Turn one on and send its variants for approval  3) Something else
Coordinator: 1.
Ad tool:     All off. Asset BK-114: "Vendor photo shoot, no AI, vendor statement on file",
             approved by J. Ortiz. Published.
Ad tool:     Live check, 1 hour later: Feed and Stories renders match BK-114. Logged.
```
In the fixed version the enhancements are caught on the duplicate, the person chooses, the asset carries its origin and approver, and the live ad is checked against the approved file.

## 3. Why it happens
- **The platform edits the image after approval.** REI said Meta "auto-enrolled" it in an AI personalization tool that altered a vendor image. An agency blog says Meta's Advantage+ is many separate tools, and that duplicating or editing an ad can turn recommended enhancements back on. The team approved one file; a different image ran.
- **Review stops at the file, not the live ad.** The checks a team runs look at the asset in the shared drive. Nobody looks at what shoppers see. A Meta spokesperson said advertisers who use its full image generation feature can review images first, but one brand said it could not preview the ad that ran (Shopifreaks).
- **AI comes in through vendors and standard tools.** Wizards of the Coast said AI parts of tools like Photoshop "crept into our marketing creative" from a vendor. Wacom bought images through a vendor and checked them with online detectors that said they were not AI. A rule saying "no AI" with no record and no vendor statement cannot be checked.

## 5. The fix
**The principle:** The image a customer sees is the image a named person approved, and the business knows whether AI made or changed it.

**Do this: brands, agencies and marketplaces**
1. **Approve the exact version that runs.** Every published image (ad render, listing photo, email, report figure) has a named approver and date in the asset system, for that version. A new crop, variant or edit is a new version.
2. **Tag the origin of every asset.** Required field: photo shoot, stock, vendor-made, AI-generated or AI-edited, with the tool name. "Unknown" cannot be published.
3. **Get a written AI statement from vendors.** Each vendor or stock asset comes with the vendor's statement of whether AI tools made or edited it. Do not use an online AI detector as the check; Wacom's said "not AI" (section 4).
4. **Keep platform AI enhancements off by default.** Turn them off at the account level and again in each ad, since one switch does not cover the others (JumpFly).
5. **Re-check enhancements on every publish.** Duplicating, replacing a creative, changing format or editing an ad triggers a fresh check of every enhancement before it goes live.
6. **Approve every AI variant before it runs.** If the team turns an enhancement on, each variant the platform makes goes to the approver. A variant nobody saw does not run.
7. **Check the live ad against the approved file.** Within 24 hours of launch and after each edit, capture each placement's render and compare it with the approved asset. A difference not on the allowed list (platform resize, crop to placement) pauses the ad.
8. **Audit settings weekly.** Export enhancement settings for every live ad and flag any switched on without an approval record.
9. **Disclose by policy, from the record.** Label AI-made or materially AI-edited assets according to the brand's written disclosure policy. Before any public statement about AI use, check the asset record and the vendor statement.
10. **Route customer reports fast.** A comment or review that says an ad image looks AI-made or wrong goes to the campaign owner the same business day.

**Reference design: asset record (one row per published version)**
```yaml
asset_id: BK-114-v2
origin: vendor_photo_shoot          # photo_shoot | stock | vendor_made | ai_generated | ai_edited
ai_tools: []                        # e.g. [image_expansion] if a platform enhancement made this version
vendor_ai_statement: on_file_2026-03-28
approved_by: J. Ortiz
approved_on: 2026-04-02
placements: [ig_feed, ig_stories]
platform_enhancements: {image_expansion: off, background_generation: off, text_variations: off}
live_check: {date: 2026-04-02, result: match}
disclosure_label: none_required_by_policy
```

**Moves** (see [conversation moves](../../../../human-machine-interface/solutions-library/conversation-moves.md); most preferred first)
| Move | When, in this pattern |
|---|---|
| Offer choices: 2-4 options plus "Something else", free text open | The ad tool finds enhancements switched on at publish: keep off, approve variants, something else |
| Ask one clarifying question | An asset's origin field is blank: "Was this from a shoot, stock, a vendor, or made with AI?" |
| Admit uncertainty and show how to check | The tool cannot tell if a stock or vendor image used AI: say so and point to the vendor statement, not a detector score |
| Hand off to a person | The live ad does not match the approved file, or a customer reports an AI-looking image |

**Don't over-correct.** AI images are allowed when a person approved them and the record says so. Platform resizing and cropping to fit a placement are fine if they are on the allowed list. Ordinary retouching, such as color correction, is not "AI-edited" unless a generative tool changed content. Do not pause every ad on one "is this AI?" comment; compare the live render with the approved file first. Do not ban Photoshop or ask vendors to prove a negative; ask for a written statement.

**Hand-off rule.** A live ad that does not match its approved file, or a customer report about an AI-looking image, goes to the campaign owner with the asset record, the settings export and the live capture. The ad is paused until that person decides. A public statement about AI use comes only after the owner and, for vendor assets, the vendor have checked the record.

**Framework used:** none yet. Rules 4 and 5 follow the JumpFly settings guidance; rule 7 answers the REI and True Classic cases; the rest is our judgment.

### Detection signals (proposed, not yet calibrated)
| Signal | Where it shows | Counter-signal (what it could be instead) |
|---|---|---|
| An AI enhancement is on in a live ad while the account default is off | Ads Manager settings export | The team turned it on on purpose and approved each variant |
| The live render differs from the approved file beyond resize or crop | Post-launch capture vs. asset system | A placement crop on the allowed list |
| Comments say "AI," "fake" or name a defect ("two handlebars") | Ad comment feed, listing reviews | A general joke about AI, not about this image |
| The platform shows an "AI info" label on an ad whose asset is tagged "no AI" | Ad preview, three-dot menu | A minor tool edit left AI metadata; check the record |
| An asset is published with a blank or "unknown" origin | Asset system publish log | A legacy asset from before the field existed |

**Starter rule:** no image is published, and no ad stays live past its first check, unless a named person approved the exact version shoppers see.

## 9. Common questions
### Why did Meta change my ad image?
Meta's Advantage+ creative enhancements can change an approved image, and REI said Meta auto-enrolled it in such a tool. Check every enhancement in each ad, not only the account default.

### How do I turn off Advantage+ creative enhancements for good?
Per an agency blog, set account defaults under Ad account settings, Creative features, then check each ad again after duplicating or editing it. Meta's own help page did not load for us.

### Do I have to disclose AI-generated images in ads?
This page does not answer the legal question. Meta says it labels ads made or significantly edited with its own AI tools and is starting to detect third-party AI. Set a written brand policy and get legal review.

### How can I tell if a vendor's image was made with AI?
Ask the vendor for a written statement and the source files. Wacom checked with online detectors that said "not AI" and later was not sure how the images were made.

### Should we stop using AI images in ads?
Not necessarily. The failure is running images nobody approved. An AI image that a person checked, tagged and labeled by policy is a different case.
