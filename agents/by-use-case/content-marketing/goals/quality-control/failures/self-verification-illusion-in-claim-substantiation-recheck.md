# Self-Verification Illusion in Claim-Substantiation Recheck

## Issue: When a Quality-Control Agent Is Asked to "Fact-Check" a Statistical or Comparative Claim Already Drafted by a Content-Generation Agent, the Recheck Re-Runs the Same Underlying Model Against the Same Draft and Largely Reproduces the Original Claim's Framing, Manufacturing False Confidence Rather Than Verifying the Claim Against an Independent Source

**Frequency**: Common

**Symptoms**
- The fact-check recheck step approves the large majority of statistical and comparative claims it reviews, including claims later found by an independent audit to be unsupported or fabricated, with the recheck's stated reasoning closely paraphrasing the original draft's framing of the claim rather than tracing it to an independent source
- Confidence language in the recheck output ("verified," "well-supported," "consistent with available data") appears even when the recheck performed no independent source lookup, and the recheck's pass rate does not change when the underlying claim is silently altered to be factually false in a controlled test
- Claims rechecked by a genuinely independent process (an analyst tracing the claim to its cited source, or a separate tool-grounded citation lookup) show a materially lower approval rate than claims rechecked by the same agent re-prompted on the same draft
- The recheck rarely flags a claim for revision even on content later found by an independent audit to contain a fabricated or unsupported statistic, indicating the recheck is not functioning as a genuine substantiation check
- Published-content corrections for unsubstantiated claims disproportionately involve content that passed a same-model fact-check recheck rather than an independent source-tracing review

**Root Cause**
Asking an LLM agent to fact-check a claim by re-prompting it with the same draft does not introduce an independent source of evidence; the model has no privileged access to ground truth about whether the underlying statistic is real beyond what it already used (or fabricated) to produce the original claim, so its "recheck" is largely a restatement of the same token-level reasoning that produced the claim, often with amplified confidence language because the prompt framing ("fact-check this claim") biases the model toward confirming plausibility rather than tracing the claim back to a verifiable source. This differs from genuine substantiation, which requires either an independent citation lookup against the actual cited source or a human reviewer who did not produce the original draft.

**Example**
```
Content-generation agent drafts a comparative claim: "Our platform processes claims 40% faster than the industry average, according to a 2025 industry benchmark study"
Quality-control pipeline includes a "fact-check" step where the same underlying model is re-prompted: "Review this claim and confirm whether it is well-supported"
Recheck restates the claim's framing and concludes "Verified -- consistent with available industry benchmarking data," without retrieving or checking the actual cited 2025 study for the specific 40% figure
No such study exists with that specific figure; the number was generated by the original drafting pass as a plausible-sounding statistic, and the fact-check recheck, working from the same draft with no independent source lookup, had no mechanism to catch the fabrication
Published content is later flagged in a compliance audit for an unsubstantiated comparative claim, after passing what the workflow's records show as a completed "fact-check" step
```

**Contributing Factors**
- Fact-check recheck step re-prompts the identical model on the identical draft rather than introducing a structurally independent check (citation-source lookup, different model, or human reviewer)
- Prompt framing for the recheck ("fact-check/confirm this claim") biases the model toward confirmation of plausibility rather than toward tracing the claim back to a verifiable source
- No tracking distinguishes "verified by independent source-tracing" from "re-confirmed by same process," so the two are reported identically as a completed fact-check step

---

## Mitigation Strategies

1. **Require Structural Independence in the Recheck**: Route the fact-check pass to an independent citation-source lookup tool that retrieves and checks the actual cited source for the specific figure, a different model, or a human reviewer -- never a same-model re-prompt conditioned on the original draft's own framing
2. **Mandatory Source-Trace Before Approval**: Require every statistical or comparative claim to be traced to its specific cited source and the exact figure verified against that source before the claim can pass the fact-check step, rejecting any claim whose cited source cannot be independently located or confirmed
3. **Track Approval-Rate Divergence by Recheck Type**: Continuously measure and report the claim-approval rate separately for same-model rechecks versus independent source-traced rechecks; a large divergence is itself evidence that the same-model recheck is not functioning as verification
4. **Adversarial Substitution Testing**: Periodically inject controlled false statistics into drafts before the fact-check step to test whether the recheck catches a known-false claim, and treat a failure to catch it as a signal the recheck step is not functioning as substantiation

### Metrics
- Claim-approval rate, segmented by same-model recheck vs. independent source-traced recheck vs. human-only review
- Rate of recheck outputs that perform an independent source lookup versus those that restate the original claim's framing with no source-trace
- Detection rate of controlled false-statistic injections in adversarial substitution testing

### Alerts
- Same-model recheck approval rate exceeds independent-source-traced recheck approval rate by a material margin for two consecutive reporting periods → P1
- A published claim is found, on audit, to have passed a fact-check recheck with no independent source-trace performed → P1
- Adversarial substitution test finds the fact-check step fails to catch a known-false injected statistic → P1
