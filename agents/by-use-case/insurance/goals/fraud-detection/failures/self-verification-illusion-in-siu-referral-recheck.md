# Self-Verification Illusion in SIU Referral Recheck

## Issue: When a Fraud-Detection Agent Is Asked to "Double-Check" Its Own Special-Investigation-Unit (SIU) Referral Before Finalizing It, the Recheck Re-Runs the Same Model Against the Same Evidence and Largely Reproduces the Original Reasoning, Manufacturing False Confidence Rather Than Providing Independent Verification

**Frequency**: Common

**Symptoms**
- Recheck step confirms the original SIU referral in the large majority of cases, including ones later found by human SIU investigators to be a false positive, with the recheck's stated reasoning closely paraphrasing the original referral's reasoning rather than introducing new evidence or a different analytical angle
- Confidence language in the recheck output ("confirmed," "high confidence," "consistent with fraud indicators") increases between the first pass and the recheck even when no new evidence was introduced, despite the recheck having access to exactly the same inputs as the original determination
- Cases where the recheck is performed by a genuinely independent process (a different model, a different evidence subset, or a human investigator) show a materially different referral-confirmation rate than cases rechecked by the same agent re-prompted on the same evidence
- The recheck rarely surfaces a reason to reverse the original referral even on claims that an independent audit later finds were inappropriately referred, indicating the recheck is not functioning as a genuine error-catching step
- SIU caseload includes a disproportionate share of referrals where the "two-pass" review trail consists of two highly similar reasoning chains from the same underlying model rather than two analytically distinct evaluations

**Root Cause**
Asking an LLM agent to verify its own prior output by re-prompting it with the same evidence does not introduce an independent source of evidence or a different reasoning process; the model has no privileged access to ground truth beyond what it already used to produce the first answer, so its "recheck" is largely a restatement of the same token-level reasoning that produced the original conclusion, often with amplified confidence language because the prompt framing ("verify this referral") biases the model toward confirming rather than re-deriving the answer from first principles. This differs from genuine verification, which requires either new evidence, a structurally different evaluation method, or an independent reviewer who did not produce the original judgment.

**Example**
```
Fraud-detection agent flags a claim for SIU referral, citing a cluster of indicators: claim filed shortly after a policy change, inconsistent timeline details, and a repair estimate from a vendor previously associated with other flagged claims
Pipeline includes a "verification" step where the same agent is re-prompted: "Review this SIU referral and confirm whether the evidence supports it"
Recheck restates the same three indicators in similar language and concludes "Confirmed -- high confidence fraud referral is warranted," without retrieving any additional evidence or considering an alternative explanation for the timeline inconsistency
SIU investigator later finds the timeline inconsistency was attributable to the claimant's documented language-barrier-driven miscommunication during intake, not deception -- a distinction the recheck step, working from identical inputs to the original pass, had no mechanism to surface
```

**Contributing Factors**
- Recheck step re-prompts the identical model on identical evidence rather than introducing a structurally independent check (different model, different evidence subset, or human reviewer)
- Prompt framing for the recheck ("verify/confirm this referral") biases the model toward confirmation rather than toward re-deriving the conclusion from first principles
- No tracking distinguishes "verified by independent process" from "re-confirmed by same process," so the two are reported identically as a completed two-pass review

---

## Mitigation Strategies

1. **Require Structural Independence in the Recheck**: Route the verification pass to either a different model, a prompt that withholds the original conclusion and re-derives a judgment from raw evidence, or a human SIU reviewer -- never a same-model re-prompt conditioned on the original referral's own framing
2. **Blind Re-Evaluation**: When the recheck must use the same model, strip the original referral's conclusion and reasoning from the recheck's context and have it independently re-evaluate the raw claim evidence, then compare the two independent conclusions rather than asking the model to "confirm" a stated prior conclusion
3. **Track Confirmation-Rate Divergence by Recheck Type**: Continuously measure and report the SIU referral confirmation rate separately for same-model rechecks versus independent rechecks; a large divergence is itself evidence that the same-model recheck is not functioning as verification
4. **Outcome-Linked Calibration Audit**: Periodically compare SIU-referred claims' recheck confidence language against actual SIU investigation outcomes to test whether stated confidence is predictive of being upheld, not just self-consistent

### Metrics
- SIU referral confirmation rate, segmented by same-model recheck vs. independent recheck vs. human-only review
- Rate of recheck outputs that introduce new evidence or a new analytical angle versus those that restate the original reasoning verbatim or near-verbatim
- Correlation between recheck-stated confidence level and actual SIU investigation outcome (upheld vs. reversed)

### Alerts
- Same-model recheck confirmation rate exceeds independent-recheck confirmation rate by a material margin for two consecutive reporting periods → P1
- Recheck output found to restate original reasoning with no new evidence on a claim later reversed by SIU investigation → P2
- A new fraud-detection workflow is deployed with a same-model "verify your own referral" step and no independent-review fallback → P3
